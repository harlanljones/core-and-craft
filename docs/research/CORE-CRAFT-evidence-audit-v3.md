# CORE & CRAFT: Evidence Audit — Iteration 3

**Research checked:** 30 October 2026. **Companion:** [Iteration 3 framework](CORE-CRAFT-framework-v3.md). **Earlier versions:** [iteration 2 framework](CORE-CRAFT-framework-v2.md), [iteration 2 audit](CORE-CRAFT-evidence-audit-v2.md), [iteration 1 framework](CORE-CRAFT-framework-v1.md), and [iteration 1 original-claim audit](CORE-CRAFT-evidence-audit-v1.md).

## Scope and method

This targeted third-round audit examines each iteration 2 pillar's definition and operating rule, each numbered protocol step, and its weekly pulse. The earlier audits preserve the mapping to the original framework and the decisions made in the first two rounds. The nine pillar names and the C.O.R.E. / C.R.A.F.T. categories remain unchanged.

Research attention was concentrated on health, personal finances, and AI-agent security because incorrect advice there can cause disproportionate harm; the other pillars also received new source checks. At least three distinct sources are listed per pillar. **New** identifies a source not listed for that pillar in the iteration 2 audit; it does not mean the source was published recently. Retained sources remain relevant and link directly to the material reviewed. Source pages, abstracts, or available full text were inspected; each entry identifies the kind of evidence and an important limit. An abstract-only inspection is not a full risk-of-bias review. This is not an exhaustive or systematic review of the literature.

**Evidence verdicts.** **Supported** means evidence or authoritative guidance supports the narrower proposition stated, not the whole practice or every person. **Mixed** means the evidence is indirect, heterogeneous, context-dependent, or insufficient for the full claim. **Unsupported** means a specific assertion is not established by this review, not that it is proven false. **Values-based** identifies a preference or ethical commitment that empirical research alone cannot settle. Retain, qualify, and extend are editing decisions rather than evidence grades.

Studies of association do not by themselves prove causation. Experimental findings apply to studied populations, comparators, tasks, and settings. A clinical or government recommendation is not a universal prescription; norms from philosophy, religion, or professional codes are not scientific proof of those commitments. **The nine-pillar package, categories, protocols, and pulse have not been validated as one intervention.** Daily logs, weekly reflection, two interactions, and 90-minute focus blocks remain optional personal choices, not evidence-derived doses. Medical, mental-health, financial, legal, and spiritual choices may require individualized advice.

## Decisions that distinguish this round

1. Clarify that career autonomy does not supply the authority, time, information, or resources needed to meet changing demands; add a safe route for asking for decisions or reprioritization.
2. Distinguish connection's structure, function, and quality; add support and access without treating a conversation count as a treatment or a person's safety as negotiable.
3. Consider cash, debt, obligations, and options together: an increased balance can be a transfer or coincide with increased debt, while using a reserve during a shock is not failure. Add evidence on two savings programs and early creditor contact.
4. Add learning calibration: compare confidence and performance with credible feedback, then check transfer to a different case. A single classroom intervention does not establish professional competence.
5. Correct the potential implication that increasing activity is safe for everyone. For people with post-exertional malaise, including ME/CFS, delayed deterioration after physical **or mental** effort calls for individualized clinical advice, not pushing through a general exercise target.
6. Add controls for unsafe **outputs** as well as untrusted inputs and excessive permissions. New adversarial testing evidence is contextual, not a deployment-wide failure rate.
7. Add care and relationships as an explicitly normative ethical lens, including burdens of care and routes for redress. Protect the distinction between religious faith, secular ethics, and empirical claims.
8. Treat a plan as something to adapt when conditions change, rather than a mandate to fill available time or a causal guarantee of productivity.

### Sources — Career

- <a id="ca1"></a> **CA1 — retained.** Harkin et al. (2016), *Does Monitoring Goal Progress Promote Goal Attainment?* [University repository abstract](https://eprints.whiterose.ac.uk/id/eprint/87431/). Meta-analysis of 138 randomized studies: monitoring interventions improved goal attainment on average across heterogeneous goals; it did not test a daily career log or guarantee promotion.
- <a id="ca2"></a> **CA2 — retained.** Humphrey, Nahrgang & Morgeson (2007), *Integrating Motivational, Social, and Contextual Work Design Features*. [Full article](http://www.morgeson.com/downloads/humphrey_nahrgang_morgeson_2007.pdf). Meta-analysis of correlations across 259 studies distinguished scheduling, methods, and decision autonomy as well as social and contextual factors. Correlation does not establish a specific redesign's effect.
- <a id="ca3"></a> **CA3 — retained.** DeLine & Parnin (2010), *Evaluating Cues for Resuming Interrupted Programming Tasks*. [Institutional abstract](https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/research/publication/evaluating-cues-for-resuming-interrupted-programming-tasks/). A survey of 371 programmers and controlled lab comparison found two automated cues performed better than notes alone. It did not compare a handwritten note against no cue or establish a universal field result.
- <a id="ca4"></a> **CA4 — new.** Tummers & Bakker (2021), *Leadership and Job Demands-Resources Theory: A Systematic Review*. [Full review](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8514935/). Reviewed 139 studies connecting leadership, demands, and resources; 74% were cross-sectional. The review maps organizational factors and methodological limitations, not a causal guarantee that asking for resources changes an individual's career.
- <a id="ca5"></a> **CA5 — retained.** Nobel (2011), *How Small Wins Unleash Creativity*. [Harvard Business School article](https://www.library.hbs.edu/working-knowledge/how-small-wins-unleash-creativity). Reports Amabile and Kramer's work-diary research, including examples of changing goals and resource constraints. Diary associations and interviews do not prove logging causes career advancement.

### Sources — Outlook & Emotional Health

- <a id="oh1"></a> **OH1 — retained.** Butler & Kern (2016), *The PERMA-Profiler: A brief multidimensional measure of flourishing*. [Original article](https://www.internationaljournalofwellbeing.org/index.php/ijow/article/view/526); [authors' scoring instructions](https://www.peggykern.org/uploads/5/6/6/7/56678211/the_perma-profiler_101416.pdf). General well-being measure with separate positive and negative emotion; the authors' overall score averages 15 PERMA items plus happiness, not negative emotion. Brief scales and general wording limit event-specific interpretation; article and instructions count as one source.
- <a id="oh2"></a> **OH2 — retained.** Patterson, Sagui-Henson & Prather (2020), *Measures of Psychosocial Stress and Stressful Exposures*. [Full review](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8063511/). Selected-measures review for adult rheumatology research: PSS-10 asks about perceived stress over the past month and is not diagnostic. Generalizability and translation can vary; consult the current rights-holder information in [OH7](#oh7).
- <a id="oh3"></a> **OH3 — retained.** Fernandes & Tone (2021), *A systematic review and meta-analysis of the association between expressive suppression and positive affect*. [PubMed abstract](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/34325115/). Forty-one effect sizes showed an overall negative association, with variation by culture and type of emotion. Suppressing outward expression is not the same as denying a feeling; associations do not require disclosure.
- <a id="oh4"></a> **OH4 — retained.** World Health Organization (2020), *Doing What Matters in Times of Stress*. [Institutional guidance](https://www.who.int/publications/i/item/9789240003927). Offers evidence-informed self-help skills; it is not a trial of every exercise or a substitute for professional care.
- <a id="oh5"></a> **OH5 — retained.** Scullin et al. (2018), *The effects of bedtime writing on difficulty falling asleep*. [PubMed abstract](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/29058942/). Fifty-seven healthy young adults were randomized to future-task or completed-task writing before one lab night. No no-writing control or chronic-insomnia sample; application to work shutdown is indirect.
- <a id="oh6"></a> **OH6 — retained.** National Institute of Mental Health (reviewed 2026), *Caring for Your Mental Health*. [Institutional guidance](https://www.nimh.nih.gov/health/topics/caring-for-your-mental-health). Describes individualized self-care, professional support, and urgent help. Examples are not a test of the framework; U.S. crisis numbers are jurisdiction-specific and people may seek help earlier.
- <a id="oh7"></a> **OH7 — retained.** Carnegie Mellon University, Cohen laboratory, *Scales: Perceived Stress Scale*. [Instrument and permission information](https://www.cmu.edu/dietrich/psychology/stress-immunity-disease-lab/scales/index.html). The authors state the PSS is not diagnostic and has no score cutoffs, and direct permission requests to the named platform. Check current use and translation requirements.
- <a id="oh8"></a> **OH8 — new.** Campos-Paíno et al. (2023), *Effectiveness of social support-based interventions in preventing depression in people without clinical depression*. [PubMed abstract](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/36419332/). Nine randomized trials (927 participants without baseline clinical depression) found an average depressive-symptom benefit, but heterogeneity was substantial, risk of bias moderate or high, and GRADE certainty low. Structured interventions are not equivalent to any particular informal conversation or evidence of treatment efficacy for diagnosed depression.

### Sources — Relationships & Connections

- <a id="rc1"></a> **RC1 — retained.** Holt-Lunstad, Smith & Layton (2010), *Social Relationships and Mortality Risk*. [PubMed abstract](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/20668659/). Meta-analysis of 148 prospective studies associated stronger relationships with survival; observational designs and heterogeneous measures do not establish causation or a contact quota.
- <a id="rc2"></a> **RC2 — retained.** Lincoln (2000), *Social Support, Negative Social Interactions, and Psychological Well-Being*. [Full critical review](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4651456/). Reviews 28 studies with positive and negative interactions and inconsistent findings. It cannot prove that negative interactions always outweigh support.
- <a id="rc3"></a> **RC3 — retained.** Lasgaard et al. (2026), *Are loneliness interventions effective for reducing loneliness?* [PubMed abstract](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/41129341/). Meta-analysis of 280 studies included 122 randomized trials; average short-term results favored varied interventions, but certainty was low or very low. It cannot determine reliably who benefits most or establish an informal-contact quota.
- <a id="rc4"></a> **RC4 — retained.** Blodgett et al. (2025), *What works to reduce loneliness: a rapid systematic review of 101 interventions*. [Full review](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12119363/). Defines loneliness as an unwanted mismatch in connection, distinct from isolation. Many studies lacked controls or randomization; English-language and OECD restrictions limit generalizability.
- <a id="rc5"></a> **RC5 — new.** World Health Organization (2025), *Social connection*. [Questions and answers](https://www.who.int/news-room/questions-and-answers/item/social-connection). Defines structure, function, and quality, distinguishes loneliness and isolation, and describes barriers and community-level options. Institutional synthesis, not an individual intervention trial; health associations are not proof that a particular contact causes an outcome.

### Sources — Economic Security & Finances

- <a id="ef1"></a> **EF1 — retained.** Consumer Financial Protection Bureau, *An essential guide to building an emergency fund*. [Institutional guidance](https://www.consumerfinance.gov/an-essential-guide-to-building-an-emergency-fund/). A safe, accessible reserve can help with shocks; amounts depend on circumstances and automated transfers require balance checks. U.S. context.
- <a id="ef2"></a> **EF2 — new.** CFPB Office of Research (2020), *Evidence-Based Strategies to Build Emergency Savings*. - <a id="ef3"></a> **EF3 — new.** Pomeranz & Kast (2024), *Savings Accounts to Borrow Less: Experimental Evidence from Chile*. [Journal abstract](https://jhr.uwpress.org/content/59/1/70). - <a id="ef4"></a> **EF4 — new.** Azurdia & Freedman (2016), *Encouraging Nonretirement Savings at Tax Time: Final Impact Findings from the SaveUSA Evaluation*. - <a id="ef6"></a> **EF6 — new.** Consumer Financial Protection Bureau (reviewed 2026), *What should I do if I can’t pay my credit card bills?* [Institutional guidance](https://www.consumerfinance.gov/ask-cfpb/what-should-i-do-if-i-cant-pay-my-credit-card-bills-en-1697/). Recommends contacting the issuer promptly, considering counseling, and checking warning signs of debt-relief scams. It concerns U.S. credit cards; availability, law, and other debt types differ.
- <a id="ef7"></a> **EF7 — new.** Consumer Financial Protection Bureau, *Financial well-being resources*. [Institutional definition and tools](https://www.consumerfinance.gov/consumer-tools/educator-tools/financial-well-being-resources). Defines perceived financial well-being through control, shock absorption, goals, and choice. Perceptions are important but are not identical to objective resources; the CFPB scale is not the CORE & CRAFT pulse.
- <a id="ef8"></a> **EF8 — retained.** U.S. Securities and Exchange Commission, *Beginners’ Guide to Asset Allocation, Diversification, and Rebalancing*. [Investor guidance](https://www.investor.gov/additional-resources/general-resources/publications-research/info-sheets/beginners-guide-asset). - <a id="ef9"></a> **EF9 — retained.** U.S. Securities and Exchange Commission, *Pay Off Credit Cards or Other High Interest Debt*. [Investor guidance](https://www.investor.gov/introduction-investing/investing-basics/save-and-invest/pay-credit-cards-or-other-high-interest). - <a id="ef10"></a> **EF10 — retained.** CFPB Office of Research (2021), *Balancing savings and debt: Findings from an online experiment*. [Research brief](https://www.consumerfinance.gov/data-research/research-reports/balancing-savings-and-debt-findings-from-an-online-experiment/). ### Sources — Competence & Mastery

- <a id="cm1"></a> **CM1 — retained.** Dunlosky et al. (2013), *Improving Students’ Learning With Effective Learning Techniques*. [PubMed abstract](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/26173288/). Review rated practice testing and distributed practice highly across educational conditions; transfer to professional engineering judgment is not assured.
- <a id="cm2"></a> **CM2 — retained.** Pan & Rickard (2018), *Transfer of test-enhanced learning: Meta-analytic review and synthesis*. [PubMed abstract](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/29733621/). - <a id="cm3"></a> **CM3 — retained.** Chernikova et al. (2020), *Simulation-Based Learning in Higher Education: A Meta-Analysis*. - <a id="cm4"></a> **CM4 — retained.** Macnamara, Hambrick & Oswald (2014), *Deliberate practice and performance in music, games, sports, education, and professions*. [PubMed abstract](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/24986855/). Practice–performance associations varied by domain; a published erratum exists. The findings do not establish a universal practice dose or prove practice alone causes expertise.
- <a id="cm5"></a> **CM5 — new.** Cogliano, Bernacki & Kardash (2021), *A Metacognitive Retrieval Practice Intervention to Improve Undergraduates’ Monitoring and Control Processes and Use of Performance Feedback for Classroom Learning*. [ERIC abstract](https://eric.ed.gov/?id=EJ1318830). ### Sources — Time Management & Planning

- <a id="tm1"></a> **TM1 — retained.** Albulescu et al. (2022), *“Give me a break!”* [Full review](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9432722/). - <a id="tm2"></a> **TM2 — retained.** Chauhan et al. (2025), *Chronotype and synchrony effects in human cognitive performance*. [PubMed abstract](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/40293205/). - <a id="tm3"></a> **TM3 — retained.** Guo et al. (2021), *Effects of interventions to reduce the negative consequences of interruptions on task performance*. [PubMed abstract](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/34273814/). Review of 33 laboratory experiments found effects on accuracy and resumption lag that varied by intervention and task. - <a id="tm4"></a> **TM4 — retained.** Neubauer & Freudenthaler (1995), *Ultradian rhythms in cognitive performance: no evidence for a 1.5-h rhythm*. - <a id="tm6"></a> **TM6 — new.** Aeon, Faber & Panaccio (2021), *Does time management work? A meta-analysis*. ## Remaining uncertainties

- The full CORE & CRAFT package and its combined review have not been tested or validated. Evidence for a component is not evidence for an overall score, prescribed routine, or personal outcome.
- PERMA is a general well-being instrument; PSS-10 concerns the past month. Neither is an acute-event diagnosis or prerequisite for support.
- Loneliness interventions vary and have low- or very-low-certainty evidence. Contact quantity alone does not establish safety, quality, or effective treatment.
- The two savings experiments tested distinct offers in specific populations. They do not identify a universal reserve/debt ordering or establish that accumulating savings alone removes hardship.
- Chronic-insomnia guidance concerns diagnosed adults; the ME/CFS source is informational clinical-care material, not a federal treatment guideline. General activity targets must not be used to force exertion despite post-exertional worsening.
- Learning, time-management and interruption studies often use students, surveys or laboratories. Transfer and causal effects in professional work remain uncertain.
- AI results depend on task, system, model and date. Security competitions deliberately search for attacks; neither a successful attack count nor a clean test is an ordinary deployment incident rate or a proof of security.
- Moral and religious traditions differ. Ethical lenses and professional codes offer reasons and responsibilities, not scientific proof, a shared creed, or a mechanical resolution of every conflict.
