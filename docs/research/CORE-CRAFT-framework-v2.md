# The CORE & CRAFT Personal Framework — Iteration 2

## A dual-category framework for life foundations, career execution, and long-term growth

**C.O.R.E. — Foundations & Capital** organizes career direction, emotional well-being, relationships, and financial security. **C.R.A.F.T. — Execution, Systems & Growth** organizes learning, physical recovery, tools, ethical grounding, and time. The names and nine pillars are unchanged. These categories are a navigation aid, not a scientific hierarchy: recovery and ethics are foundations too, and no person's value depends on productivity.

This iteration expands the definitions, distinguishes useful routines from tested interventions, and strengthens boundaries around measurement, health, financial tradeoffs, and agentic systems. It is educational, not individualized medical, mental-health, financial, legal, or spiritual advice. The nine-pillar package has **not** been tested as a combined intervention. Bracketed source IDs link to the [iteration 2 evidence audit](CORE-CRAFT-evidence-audit-v2.md), where each source's scope, uncertainty, and the revision from iteration 1 are documented. The [iteration 1 framework](CORE-CRAFT-framework-v1.md) and [audit](CORE-CRAFT-evidence-audit-v1.md) remain unchanged.

### How to use it

- Start with the practices that fit your current health, resources, work, obligations, beliefs, and access. Adapt or skip the rest. A missed practice, necessary care work, or a hard week is information—not failure.
- A daily note, a weekly review, a two-interaction reminder, or a 90-minute block is a personal choice, not a validated dose. Population guidelines are labeled with their intended audience and are not mandatory entry tests.
- Attend to urgent needs when they arise; do not wait for the next pulse. Protect safety, dignity, sleep, financial obligations, and relationships when seeking leverage.
- Keep personal notes private and proportionate. If tracking or writing increases rumination, distress, or risk, stop or change the practice. Organizational and structural constraints cannot always be solved by individual planning.

# Part 1: C.O.R.E. — Foundations & Capital

## C — Career

**Definition.** Direct professional effort toward meaningful outcomes and sustainable advancement. Career capital includes practical skills, trusted working relationships, a credible record of contributions, and opportunities to exercise judgment. It encompasses maintenance, enabling work, collaboration, learning, and care—not only shipped features or promotions. Autonomy can mean discretion over methods, timing, or decisions; it varies with role, resources, and organizational authority. Advancement is affected by other people's decisions and opportunities outside individual control. [CA2](CORE-CRAFT-evidence-audit-v2.md#ca2) [CA5](CORE-CRAFT-evidence-audit-v2.md#ca5)

**Operating rule.** Protect feasible attention for an agreed outcome; make progress, blockers, and changes visible without mistaking activity or manufactured wins for impact. Monitoring progress has evidence across varied goals, but does not guarantee recognition or promotion. [CA1](CORE-CRAFT-evidence-audit-v2.md#ca1)

**Protocol.**

1. **Agree on the work.** Name the intended outcome and beneficiary, the next meaningful milestone, what success would look like, and who can decide scope or resolve a blocker. Clarify required resources, constraints, and how feedback will be obtained when possible.
2. **Create feasible focus.** Reserve a task-suited block when the work allows; agree on a route for urgent matters and break or stop for fatigue and obligations. Ninety minutes is one optional experiment, not a biological optimum. If interrupted, a brief note or tool-generated context cue may help you resume; choose what works locally. [CA3](CORE-CRAFT-evidence-audit-v2.md#ca3) [TM4](CORE-CRAFT-evidence-audit-v2.md#tm4)
3. **Make the record useful.** At shutdown or another convenient time, record a completed step, learning, decision, or blocker and the next action. A short daily entry is an optional default; no win needs to be invented. If an agreement changes, note the prior expectation and change, then seek clarification from the person with authority to resolve it.
4. **Communicate responsibly.** Preserve permitted, non-confidential evidence of contributions and share relevant results, career goals, or requests for feedback when useful. Credit collaborators fairly; visibility is not a substitute for organizational support.

## O — Outlook & Emotional Health

**Definition.** Notice emotional experiences and sources of stress, respond in ways suited to the situation, recover psychologically, and seek support when useful. This includes positive experiences, difficult feelings, functioning, and the circumstances that affect them. It is not a demand for constant positivity or the absence of anxiety, sadness, or anger; how and whether to express an emotion depends on culture, context, and safety. [OH3](CORE-CRAFT-evidence-audit-v2.md#oh3)

**Operating rule.** Treat emotions as information, not personal failures. Separate a stressful event from general well-being and from stress perceived over a longer period. Observation is useful only if it supports action or understanding; it is not an obligation to disclose, diagnose, or continually monitor yourself.

**Protocol.**

1. **Notice and respond.** When useful, note a stressor, emotional response, effect on daily functioning, and what helped or made recovery harder. Consider a feasible next step: change or reduce a demand, take a grounding or rest break, ask for practical help, or seek support. Brief event notes or ratings are informal personal observations, not validated acute-stress scores. [OH4](CORE-CRAFT-evidence-audit-v2.md#oh4)
2. **Choose measurement only if useful.** The optional PERMA-Profiler concerns **general well-being**: its positive- and negative-emotion scales are separate, and neither is an acute-stress measure. The optional PSS-10 asks about perceived stress over the **past month**, not a specific event; weekly scores would have overlapping recall periods. Use an instrument's original wording and scoring and check current permissions. Neither instrument diagnoses a condition or supplies a clinical cutoff. The PERMA authors' overall score averages the 15 PERMA items and the separate happiness item; it does not subtract the negative-emotion scale. [OH1](CORE-CRAFT-evidence-audit-v2.md#oh1) [OH2](CORE-CRAFT-evidence-audit-v2.md#oh2) [OH7](CORE-CRAFT-evidence-audit-v2.md#oh7)
3. **Close open loops if it helps.** At shutdown, optionally list unresolved work, the next action, and where to return to it. A small bedtime-writing study compared two kinds of writing rather than a work-shutdown routine; this is a personal experiment, not a treatment for insomnia. Stop or adjust if it increases rumination. [OH5](CORE-CRAFT-evidence-audit-v2.md#oh5)
4. **Get support without a prerequisite score.** If distress persists, worsens, or interferes with life, contact a qualified professional or trusted source of support. You may seek help sooner. For immediate danger or urgent safety concerns, contact appropriate local emergency or crisis services rather than waiting for a weekly review. [OH6](CORE-CRAFT-evidence-audit-v2.md#oh6)

## R — Relationships & Connections

**Definition.** Cultivate safe, meaningful ties with family, chosen family, friends, peers, and communities, while respecting boundaries and individual needs for solitude. Connection can include belonging, shared experience, listening, giving and receiving care, and practical help. Reciprocity need not be immediate or identical, especially in caregiving. Loneliness is an unwanted gap between the connection one has and wants; social isolation concerns limited social contact. Being alone by choice is not the same as loneliness. [RC2](CORE-CRAFT-evidence-audit-v2.md#rc2) [RC4](CORE-CRAFT-evidence-audit-v2.md#rc4)

**Operating rule.** Make space for relationships that are not merely transactional, without assuming every worthwhile interaction feels energizing. Prioritize mutual respect and safety; stronger social relationships are associated with health in observational studies, but a particular number of contacts is not established. [RC1](CORE-CRAFT-evidence-audit-v2.md#rc1) [RC2](CORE-CRAFT-evidence-audit-v2.md#rc2)

**Protocol.**

1. **Notice quality and safety.** Consider where connection is supportive, where care is needed, and where more distance or a boundary would help. Directly confronting someone is not always safe; seek trusted outside support or a safer option when needed.
2. **Make connection accessible.** If desired, arrange a mutually welcome check-in, shared activity, or exchange of support by message, call, in person, or another accessible format. Community groups or qualified support are options when existing ties do not meet your needs. Structured loneliness interventions show promising average results, but the certainty is low or very low; an informal check-in is not equivalent to a tested treatment. [RC3](CORE-CRAFT-evidence-audit-v2.md#rc3)
3. **Use reminders without quotas.** Two meaningful interactions in a week can be a personal reminder if useful, not a validated health threshold or pass/fail score. Difficult, caring conversations can matter, and access, capacity, disability, safety, and chosen solitude affect what is appropriate.

## E — Economic Security & Finances

**Definition.** Understand income, cash flow, essential obligations, debt, accessible reserves, and—when appropriate—long-term assets. Economic security means having some capacity to meet commitments and respond to shocks; reducing administrative friction is helpful but cannot by itself overcome inadequate income, unequal access, or structural insecurity.

**Operating rule.** Attend to essentials and legal obligations while balancing liquidity, borrowing costs, available benefits, and longer-term goals. Automate only what is affordable and monitor it. Neither research nor general guidance establishes one universal order or percentage for emergency savings, expensive debt, and investing. [EF1](CORE-CRAFT-evidence-audit-v2.md#ef1) [EF2](CORE-CRAFT-evidence-audit-v2.md#ef2) [EF3](CORE-CRAFT-evidence-audit-v2.md#ef3)

**Protocol.**

1. **Map obligations and timing.** Note income dates, essential expenses, due dates, debt minimums and interest rates, and likely shortfalls. If funds will not cover everything, consider consequences for housing, income, insurance, and legal obligations; contact creditors or an appropriate counselor. Rules and available assistance depend on jurisdiction. A budget cannot create missing income. [EF6](CORE-CRAFT-evidence-audit-v2.md#ef6)
2. **Balance resilience and debt.** Choose an accessible emergency reserve appropriate to your situation, even if starting small. Weigh a buffer against high-interest debt, required payments, employer benefits, and other obligations; there is no one-size-fits-all sequence. If feasible, automate a modest transfer when funds are available, use balance alerts, and pause or change it to avoid an overdraft. A reserve is there to use for a shock and rebuild when possible. [EF1](CORE-CRAFT-evidence-audit-v2.md#ef1) [EF3](CORE-CRAFT-evidence-audit-v2.md#ef3)
3. **Consider long-term investing when appropriate.** Review goals, time horizon, risk tolerance, fees, taxes, debt costs, and any applicable employer match before choosing an allocation. Diversification does not eliminate loss; no security or allocation is prescribed. Consult qualified help when the tradeoffs exceed your expertise. [EF3](CORE-CRAFT-evidence-audit-v2.md#ef3) [EF5](CORE-CRAFT-evidence-audit-v2.md#ef5)
4. **Review automation.** Check upcoming bills, balances, alerts, and failed transfers on a cadence that fits pay and obligations. Review longer-term investments less often as appropriate and after material changes. Defaults, job changes, withdrawals and opt-outs can change results; automation does not guarantee security. [EF4](CORE-CRAFT-evidence-audit-v2.md#ef4)

# Part 2: C.R.A.F.T. — Execution, Systems & Growth

## C — Competence & Mastery

**Definition.** Develop knowledge, technical skill, and judgment through intentional study, realistic practice, feedback, reflection, and application. Competence includes knowing the limits of an idea, recognizing uncertainty, and adapting knowledge to unfamiliar situations. Expertise depends on domain, prior knowledge, opportunities, feedback, and more than practice hours. Career execution delivers current work; competence development increases what you can understand and do over time. [CM3](CORE-CRAFT-evidence-audit-v2.md#cm3) [CM4](CORE-CRAFT-evidence-audit-v2.md#cm4)

**Operating rule.** Protect intentional learning from being entirely displaced by reactive work, while recognizing that work tasks can also teach when they include deliberate practice and feedback. No universal study dose, career outcome, or automatic transfer from classroom exercises is established. [CM1](CORE-CRAFT-evidence-audit-v2.md#cm1) [CM2](CORE-CRAFT-evidence-audit-v2.md#cm2)

**Protocol.**

1. **Choose a target.** Pick a skill, question, paper, model, or architecture tradeoff with relevance beyond the immediate task. Reserve a recurring learning opportunity when feasible; weekly is a scheduling preference, not a minimum.
2. **Study actively and get feedback.** Study, then explain or reconstruct the idea without looking, solve a representative problem, or practice in a realistic simulation. Compare against references, tests, or knowledgeable feedback. Use examples when starting and reflection as knowledge grows; match difficulty and safeguards to the task. [CM1](CORE-CRAFT-evidence-audit-v2.md#cm1) [CM3](CORE-CRAFT-evidence-audit-v2.md#cm3)
3. **Revisit and test transfer.** Return to important material after an interval. Try a different case or realistic application, identify where assumptions fail, and seek feedback before relying on a skill in a high-stakes setting. Practice testing can improve transfer on average in studied educational tasks, but transfer varies and must be checked locally. [CM2](CORE-CRAFT-evidence-audit-v2.md#cm2)

## R — Recovery & Physical Health

**Definition.** Support physical health and a good life through sleep, accessible movement, nourishment, recovery, and medical care when needed. Rest has value in itself, not merely as fuel for work. Disability, illness, medication, age, caregiving, resources, and access shape what is realistic.

**Operating rule.** Protect sleep opportunity and regular movement as foundations, not the routine buffer for work overflow. Use population guidance as context, adapt for ability and circumstances, and do not interpret a missed target as failure. [RH1](CORE-CRAFT-evidence-audit-v2.md#rh1) [RH3](CORE-CRAFT-evidence-audit-v2.md#rh3)

**Protocol.**

1. **Protect sleep and seek care when needed.** Aim for a consistent opportunity to sleep where possible. CDC guidance recommends at least seven hours of actual sleep for adults aged 18–60; recommendations and individual needs differ by age and health. Discuss persistent sleep difficulties with a healthcare professional. For adults diagnosed with chronic insomnia, clinical guidance strongly recommends multicomponent cognitive behavioral therapy for insomnia (CBT-I) and advises against sleep hygiene as the sole treatment; ask a clinician about appropriate options rather than self-diagnosing. [RH1](CORE-CRAFT-evidence-audit-v2.md#rh1) [RH2](CORE-CRAFT-evidence-audit-v2.md#rh2)
2. **Move in accessible ways.** General CDC adult guidance is 150 minutes of moderate activity or 75 minutes of vigorous activity per week, plus muscle strengthening on at least two days. Some activity is better than none. Adapt to ability, condition, and clinical advice; a daily workout is not required. [RH3](CORE-CRAFT-evidence-audit-v2.md#rh3)
3. **Nourish.** Seek adequate, balanced, varied food, adapted to health needs, culture, preferences, and resources. There is no single prescribed menu. [RH4](CORE-CRAFT-evidence-audit-v2.md#rh4)
4. **Make recovery easier.** Try shutting down the development environment, turning devices off or moving them away from bed, or staging activity gear. These are optional environmental cues, not guaranteed treatments. Repetition and cues may help some habits, but formation varies and has no universal deadline. [RH5](CORE-CRAFT-evidence-audit-v2.md#rh5) [RH6](CORE-CRAFT-evidence-audit-v2.md#rh6)

## A — Agents & Systems

**Definition.** Design and maintain workflows and toolchains that reduce observed operational friction without surrendering quality, security, privacy, or human accountability. A deterministic script follows explicit rules; a conversational assistant suggests content for a person to assess; a tool-using agent can take actions through connected systems; and orchestration coordinates multiple tools or agents. These are different capabilities and risks, not interchangeable labels.

**Operating rule.** Delegate bounded, verifiable work only when the result is useful **and** risk is acceptable. Time saved is not permission to compromise safety, privacy, or obligations. Evidence differs by work and tool: an assisted customer-support deployment improved a measured outcome in one firm, a 2025 developer trial found a slowdown for its users, and a 2026 follow-up warned that selection and concurrent-work measurement made its estimate unreliable. None establishes the value of your current agent workflow. [AS1](CORE-CRAFT-evidence-audit-v2.md#as1) [AS2](CORE-CRAFT-evidence-audit-v2.md#as2) [AS3](CORE-CRAFT-evidence-audit-v2.md#as3)

**Protocol.**

1. **Define the job and baseline.** Describe the current process, task mix, intended output, quality checks, responsible person, known failure modes, and unacceptable harms. Choose the simplest suitable approach, including a human process or deterministic script where sufficient; a repeated task is not automatically suitable for an agent.
2. **Bound authority and data.** Give a scoped identity only the minimum access and data the task needs; use read-only access where sufficient. Enforce permissions and authorization in the downstream system, not only in the prompt. Treat retrieved pages, messages, tool results, and agent reports as untrusted content. Require an approval gate outside the agent before consequential or irreversible actions; arrange a way to stop and, where feasible, roll back. Human review is fallible and no control guarantees safety. [AS4](CORE-CRAFT-evidence-audit-v2.md#as4) [AS5](CORE-CRAFT-evidence-audit-v2.md#as5)
3. **Compare on representative work.** Run a bounded trial and compare reasonably similar tasks with the prior workflow. Record setup, oversight, verification, rework, direct and recurring costs, output quality, incidents, and failed or abandoned tasks—not only successes. Track hands-on human time separately from elapsed wall time; concurrent waiting need not be added as human labor. An informal local comparison informs a decision but is not a causal research estimate. [AS3](CORE-CRAFT-evidence-audit-v2.md#as3)
4. **Keep, change, or stop.** Assess benefit and risk separately against the agreed thresholds; do not trade an unacceptable incident for speed. Stop and address a serious failure immediately, and reassess after material changes to models, tools, permissions, data, or task type. Verify that review and safety checks actually catch relevant mistakes.

## F — Faith & Ethics

**Definition.** Ground choices and long-term purpose in the religious, moral, philosophical, and professional commitments you hold or are examining, then apply them to particular situations with judgment and accountability. Faith may be religious, questioning, secular, or expressed through another tradition. The pillar does not require disclosing a creed, adopting one of the sources in the audit, or treating scientific results as proof of a moral or religious doctrine. [FE5](CORE-CRAFT-evidence-audit-v2.md#fe5) [FE6](CORE-CRAFT-evidence-audit-v2.md#fe6)

**Operating rule.** Let chosen commitments guide ambition, technical output, relationships, and ordinary decisions. Listen to affected people where safe, recognize conflicts among obligations, seek counsel, change course, and repair harm when possible. Ethical traditions offer distinct lenses rather than one mechanical decision algorithm. [FE1](CORE-CRAFT-evidence-audit-v2.md#fe1) [FE4](CORE-CRAFT-evidence-audit-v2.md#fe4)

**Protocol.**

1. **Identify commitments.** Name the principles that matter and their grounding if useful, privately if preferred. Your own tradition, professional responsibilities, and law may impose obligations that a short checklist cannot settle.
2. **Examine a consequential choice.** Identify who is affected and whose voice may be missing. Consider foreseeable benefits and harms, duties and respect for persons, and what integrity and practical judgment require. These consequentialist, duty-based, and virtue-oriented lenses can disagree; do not assume one cancels another. In computing, consider accessibility, fairness, privacy, confidentiality, security, and professional responsibilities. [FE1](CORE-CRAFT-evidence-audit-v2.md#fe1) [FE2](CORE-CRAFT-evidence-audit-v2.md#fe2) [FE3](CORE-CRAFT-evidence-audit-v2.md#fe3) [FE4](CORE-CRAFT-evidence-audit-v2.md#fe4)
3. **Act and remain accountable.** Choose a defensible action, seek counsel or affected people's input where safe, and document important decisions proportionately. Redesign, escalate, or decline when warranted; revise or repair if new information or harm emerges. Power and safety constraints can limit available choices, and the checklist cannot guarantee integrity.

## T — Time Management & Planning

**Definition.** Coordinate attention, calendar time, obligations, energy, transitions, interruptions, and recovery in light of priorities and real constraints. Time management supports the other pillars; it does not require filling every hour or a fixed biological peak. Some interruptions are necessary, and scheduling freedom varies.

**Operating rule.** Try demanding work at times you tend to feel alert, protect feasible transitions and recovery, and preserve an urgent-contact route. Treat preferred hours, focus lengths, and break lengths as hypotheses rather than laws; evidence for chronotype effects and micro-breaks varies by task and outcome. [TM1](CORE-CRAFT-evidence-audit-v2.md#tm1) [TM2](CORE-CRAFT-evidence-audit-v2.md#tm2)

**Protocol.**

1. **Place commitments realistically.** Calendar essential obligations, sleep opportunity, and important commitments first where feasible. Reserve room for focus, learning, connection, and recovery without requiring every pillar to have a block every day.
2. **Plan focus and re-entry.** Choose a block suited to the task and leave feasible transition or break time. A 90-minute block can be an experiment, not a proven optimum; a shorter or longer block may fit better. Decide how urgent contact reaches you and, before a planned switch when possible, leave a next action or other context cue for resumption. The interruption studies do not prove any one cue works for everyone. [TM3](CORE-CRAFT-evidence-audit-v2.md#tm3) [TM4](CORE-CRAFT-evidence-audit-v2.md#tm4) [TM5](CORE-CRAFT-evidence-audit-v2.md#tm5)
3. **Observe and adjust.** Compare work, fatigue, and recovery over several occasions if useful. Consider sleep, task difficulty, interruptions, and obligations before attributing a difference to a biological rhythm. Adjust what is within your control without sacrificing sleep to chase a supposed peak.

# Weekly C.R.A.F.T. & C.O.R.E. Pulse Check

Use Sunday or another convenient day. This is a menu for reflection and adjustment, **not** a validated questionnaire, a composite score, or a requirement to improve in every dimension every week. Skip questions that do not apply; choose one feasible action if useful. Respond to urgent safety, financial, or health concerns immediately rather than waiting for the review.

## C.O.R.E. audit

- **[C] Career:** What meaningful outcome, enabling work, learning, or blocker emerged? What contribution, changed expectation, decision, or support request should I record, clarify, or share?
- **[O] Outlook & Emotional Health:** What stressors and feelings arose, how did they affect functioning, what helped me respond or recover, and would I benefit from support or a changeable demand being addressed?
- **[R] Relationships & Connections:** Did I have the safe connection or solitude I wanted? Is there someone I want to check in with, support, seek help from, or establish a safer boundary around?
- **[E] Economic Security & Finances:** Are upcoming essentials, debt obligations, balances, alerts, and transfers understood? Does the reserve/debt tradeoff, a failed payment, or a shortfall call for an adjustment or assistance?

## C.R.A.F.T. audit

- **[C] Competence & Mastery:** What did I study or practice, what feedback exposed a gap, and where did the idea transfer—or fail to transfer—to another case?
- **[R] Recovery & Physical Health:** How were my sleep opportunity and quality, movement, nourishment, and recovery environment? Is a barrier, persistent problem, or support need worth addressing?
- **[A] Agents & Systems:** On comparable work, did automation help after setup, review, rework, failed attempts, and cost? Were quality, permissions, privacy, and incidents acceptable; should I keep, change, or stop it?
- **[F] Faith & Ethics:** Which decision tested a commitment, who was affected or overlooked, and is safe consultation, clarification, a change, or repair needed?
- **[T] Time Management & Planning:** When did focus and recovery fit or conflict with energy and obligations? Which interruptions were necessary or negotiable, and what feasible adjustment is worth trying?

**Safety and scope.** Seek urgent local help for an immediate safety threat. For persistent health concerns, serious financial difficulty, or consequential decisions beyond your expertise, contact an appropriate qualified professional or support service. The framework cannot replace individualized care or resolve structural constraints.
