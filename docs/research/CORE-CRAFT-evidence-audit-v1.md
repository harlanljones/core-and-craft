# CORE & CRAFT: Evidence Audit — Iteration 1

**Research checked:** 29 October 2026. **Companion:** [Revised framework, iteration 1](CORE-CRAFT-framework-v1.md).

## Scope and reading guide

This is a targeted evidence review of the framework supplied for this iteration, not a systematic review of every relevant publication. The original operating rules, detailed protocols, and weekly pulse-check questions are assessed below. Evidence was weighted toward reviews, experiments, official guidance, and original texts; extra attention went to measurement, health, finances, and AI-agent risks. The linked source page, abstract, or available full text was inspected for each entry. An abstract-only inspection is identified where relevant; it cannot establish every methodological detail. Sources can count for multiple pillars when relevant.

The nine-pillar framework **as a combined intervention has not been validated by the sources reviewed**. Evidence for a component is not evidence that the complete package, a particular cadence, or a personal outcome is established. Association does not by itself prove causation; an experiment applies to its studied people, task, and setting. Official guidance is not a randomized trial. Philosophical and religious texts articulate normative commitments, not scientific evidence that the commitments are true or that an intervention works.

**Verdicts:**

- **Supported:** evidence or authoritative guidance supports the narrower proposition stated, not necessarily every detail of the original rule.
- **Mixed:** a useful component has support, but a stronger, more specific, or causal claim is uncertain or not established.
- **Unsupported:** the specific assertion or threshold is not established by the evidence reviewed; this does not prove it is false or useless.
- **Values-based:** an ethical preference, aspiration, or organizational choice, not a claim science can settle. An empirical consequence of that choice is assessed separately where appropriate.

Daily logs, weekly reviews, two interactions, fixed buffers, and 90-minute blocks are **personal operating choices**, not research-derived requirements. The 90-minute option is retained as an experiment, not a biological rule. This material is educational, not individualized medical, mental-health, legal, or financial advice.

### Career sources

- <a id="ca1"></a> **CA1 —** Harkin et al. (2016), *Does monitoring goal progress promote goal attainment?* [PubMed abstract](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/26479070/). Meta-analysis of 138 randomized studies: monitoring interventions improved goal attainment on average, with larger effects when information was recorded or reported. Heterogeneous goals and interventions do not validate a daily career log.
- <a id="ca2"></a> **CA2 —** Humphrey, Nahrgang & Morgeson (2007), *Integrating motivational, social, and contextual work design features*. [PubMed abstract](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/17845089/). Meta-analysis of 259 studies connects groups of work characteristics with worker attitudes and behaviors; the abstract does not isolate a causal effect for autonomy or sprint length.
- <a id="ca3"></a> **CA3 —** Nobel (2011), *How Small Wins Unleash Creativity*, Harvard Business School, discussing Amabile and Kramer's diary research. [Institutional article](https://www.library.hbs.edu/working-knowledge/how-small-wins-unleash-creativity). Diary reports link experienced progress with work life and describe shifting goals as obstacles; these associations do not prove logging causes advancement.
- <a id="ca4"></a> **CA4 —** Neubauer & Freudenthaler (1995), *Ultradian rhythms in cognitive performance: no evidence for a 1.5-h rhythm*. [PubMed abstract](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/7669837/). Sixty people performed one cognitive task repeatedly over nine hours; the conservative analysis found no significant 90-minute periodicity. This does not prove 90-minute blocks are ineffective or rule out all ultradian rhythms.

## O — Outlook & Emotional Health

| Original operating rule, protocol, or pulse check | Verdict and evidence | Revision decision |
|---|---|---|
| Manage stress, regulate affect, and recover psychologically. | **Values-based** goal; supportive self-care and professional help are context-dependent, not a validated single package. [OH4](#oh4) | Notice stressors and emotions; make room for recovery and support without requiring constant positivity. |
| Track emotional baselines and **acute stress** via PERMA-Profiler subscales. | **Mixed:** the original instrument measures general positive emotion and includes a separate general negative-emotion scale; it is **not an acute-stress measure**. The authors selected general rather than past-week wording. [OH1](#oh1) | If desired, use original wording and scoring for broad reflection. Log acute events and reactions separately as personal observations, not diagnoses. |
| Track acute stress versus positive-emotion trends in a weekly audit rather than suppressing negative affect. | **Mixed.** A review of *expressive* suppression found heterogeneous associations with positive affect across culture and emotion type; it does not show that expressing every feeling is safe or that suppression always harms. PERMA does not measure acute stress. [OH1](#oh1), [OH2](#oh2) | Review event notes and recovery separately from any general PERMA scores; acknowledge difficult emotions and choose expression in context. Weekly is a convenience, not a validated interval. |
| Use open-loop dumps before shutdown. | **Mixed; indirect evidence.** A small bedtime experiment compared writing future tasks with writing completed tasks, not writing versus no writing or workplace shutdown. [OH3](#oh3) | Optionally list unresolved items and next actions at shutdown; keep or change the practice based on whether it helps rather than increases rumination. |
| Weekly: manage stress effectively and keep background anxiety low. | **Mixed.** Support and reflection can be useful, but low anxiety is not a universal threshold or a condition for success. [OH2](#oh2), [OH4](#oh4) | Ask what stressors arose, what helped, and whether persistent distress is impairing life or calls for support. |

**PERMA handling.** Positive emotion is the mean of three general positive-emotion items; negative emotion is the mean of three general anxiety, anger, and sadness items, reported separately. Higher negative-emotion scores mean more reported negative emotion, not an acute-stress score. The authors' overall well-being score averages the 15 PERMA items **plus** the happiness item; negative emotion is not subtracted. Rewording items as “this week,” interpreting short-term score changes as acute stress, or using a clinical cutoff would require separate validation. Follow the [authors' use and scoring instructions](https://www.peggykern.org/uploads/5/6/6/7/56678211/the_perma-profiler_101416.pdf), including their registration and usage conditions. [OH1](#oh1)

### Outlook & Emotional Health sources

- <a id="oh1"></a> **OH1 —** Butler & Kern (2016), *The PERMA-Profiler: A brief multidimensional measure of flourishing*. [Original paper](https://www.internationaljournalofwellbeing.org/index.php/ijow/article/view/526); [instrument and scoring](https://www.peggykern.org/uploads/5/6/6/7/56678211/the_perma-profiler_101416.pdf). Measure-development and validation studies report evidence of reliability and validity for a 23-item instrument. Some samples were self-selected; short scales and general wording limit acute-event interpretation. The paper and its companion instructions are one source for counting purposes.
- <a id="oh2"></a> **OH2 —** Fernandes & Tone (2021), *A systematic review and meta-analysis of the association between expressive suppression and positive affect*. [PubMed abstract](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/34325115/). Forty-one effect sizes showed an overall negative association, with substantial moderation by culture and the emotion suppressed. Association and expressive suppression should not be equated with a causal effect of feeling negative emotions.
- <a id="oh3"></a> **OH3 —** Scullin et al. (2018), *The effects of bedtime writing on difficulty falling asleep*. [Full text](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5758411/). In one randomized laboratory night, 57 healthy young adults writing a to-do list fell asleep faster than those writing a completed-activity list. There was no no-writing group; results do not establish effects on chronic insomnia or workplace shutdown.
- <a id="oh4"></a> **OH4 —** National Institute of Mental Health (reviewed 2026), *Caring for Your Mental Health*. [Institutional guidance](https://www.nimh.nih.gov/health/topics/caring-for-your-mental-health). Describes individualized self-care and recommends professional help for severe or distressing symptoms lasting two weeks or more; urgency can warrant help sooner. It is guidance, not a trial of this framework.

### Relationships & Connections sources

- <a id="rc1"></a> **RC1 —** Holt-Lunstad, Smith & Layton (2010), *Social Relationships and Mortality Risk: A Meta-analytic Review*. [Full text](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC2910600/). Across 148 prospective studies, stronger social relationships were associated with greater survival odds. Measures and populations varied; observational synthesis does not validate a contact quota.
- <a id="rc2"></a> **RC2 —** Wang et al. (2023), *A systematic review and meta-analysis of 90 cohort studies of social isolation, loneliness and mortality*. [PubMed abstract](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/37337095/). Isolation and loneliness were each associated with mortality in cohort studies; they are distinct constructs and associations do not establish effects of a specific intervention.
- <a id="rc3"></a> **RC3 —** Lincoln (2000), *Social Support, Negative Social Interactions, and Psychological Well-Being*. [Full text](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4651456/). Critical review of 28 studies describes both positive and negative interactions and heterogeneous findings. It does not prove that negative interactions always outweigh support.

### Economic Security & Finances sources

- <a id="ef1"></a> **EF1 —** Consumer Financial Protection Bureau (page modified 2025), *An essential guide to building an emergency fund*. [Institutional guidance](https://www.consumerfinance.gov/an-essential-guide-to-building-an-emergency-fund/). A cash reserve can address unexpected expenses; automatic transfers may improve consistency but require balance checks to avoid overdraft. Amount and approach depend on circumstances.
- <a id="ef2"></a> **EF2 —** Choi et al. (2024, revised 2024), *Smaller than We Thought? The Effect of Automatic Savings Policies*. [NBER working paper and abstract](https://www.nber.org/papers/w32828). Analysis of nine 401(k) plans estimates that job separation, withdrawals, and opt-outs weaken long-run effects. This working paper is not a guarantee for any individual plan or savings product.
- <a id="ef3"></a> **EF3 —** U.S. Securities and Exchange Commission, *Beginners’ Guide to Asset Allocation, Diversification, and Rebalancing*. [Institutional guidance](https://www.investor.gov/additional-resources/general-resources/publications-research/info-sheets/beginners-guide-asset). Asset mix depends on goals, time horizon, and risk tolerance; fees, taxes, and periodic review matter. Diversification does not eliminate market loss.
- <a id="ef4"></a> **EF4 —** Mani, Mullainathan, Shafir & Zhao (2013), *Poverty impedes cognitive function*. [PubMed abstract](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/23990553/). Two studies reported cognitive-performance differences associated with financial concerns and harvest timing; the authors proposed a mental-resources mechanism. The inference and its generalizability are contested; it does not show a personal budget eliminates poverty.
- <a id="ef5"></a> **EF5 —** O’Donnell et al. (2021), *Empirical audit and review and an assessment of evidentiary value in research on the psychological consequences of scarcity*. [Full text](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8612349/). Replications across scarcity-priming studies showed successes and failures; the authors do not assign a single replication rate to the heterogeneous field. Interpret claims about cognitive bandwidth cautiously.
- <a id="ef6"></a> **EF6 —** Consumer Financial Protection Bureau, *Prioritizing bills*. [Institutional worksheet](https://files.consumerfinance.gov/f/documents/cfpb_your-money-your-goals_prioritizing-bills_tool.pdf). When bills cannot all be paid, assess consequences for housing, income, insurance, and obligations, and contact creditors or qualified counselors. U.S.-specific examples are not universal legal advice.

### Time Management & Planning sources

- <a id="tm1"></a> **TM1 —** Albulescu et al. (2022), *“Give me a break!” A systematic review and meta-analysis on the efficacy of micro-breaks for increasing well-being and performance*. [PubMed abstract](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/36044424/). Experimental and quasi-experimental studies found small average improvements in vigor and fatigue, but no statistically significant overall performance effect; task type and break duration varied.
- <a id="tm2"></a> **TM2 —** Chauhan et al. (2025), *Chronotype and synchrony effects in human cognitive performance: A systematic review*. [PubMed abstract](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/40293205/). Some studies reported better performance at times aligned with chronotype, with substantial variation by age, task, and conditions; this does not establish an individual's fixed peak from preference alone.
- <a id="tm3"></a> **TM3 —** Neubauer & Freudenthaler (1995), *Ultradian rhythms in cognitive performance: no evidence for a 1.5-h rhythm*. [PubMed abstract](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/7669837/). The same source as [CA4](#ca4), relevant to time planning as well as career. Its null 90-minute result is specific to the study, not proof that a 90-minute work block cannot help someone.
- <a id="tm4"></a> **TM4 —** Hayashi, Sato & Hori (1994), *Ultradian rhythms in task performance, self-evaluation, and EEG activity*. [PubMed abstract](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/7870505/). A repeated-measures study of ten male university students found a behavioral and subjective fluctuation at 12 cycles per day, or 120 minutes, alongside other EEG rhythms. This small study did not compare productivity under work-and-break schedules or establish a universal 90-minute optimum.

## Remaining uncertainties

- No reviewed study validates CORE & CRAFT as a whole, the proposed category boundary, or a combined nine-pillar score.
- Exact work-block, buffer, review, and social-contact frequencies are not established for this framework. Personal experimentation is preferable to biological or clinical claims.
- PERMA scores are general self-reports, not acute stress measures or diagnoses. More specific assessment requires an appropriate instrument and context.
- Much of the learning, social, scarcity, and AI evidence comes from particular populations, settings, tools, or observational designs. Findings do not determine an individual's outcome.
- The AI-agent field changes rapidly; assistance results and older tools should not be treated as reliable estimates for current autonomous deployments.
- Finance and health guidance is general and partly U.S.-specific; access, disability, age, medication, income, jurisdiction, and individual risk can change the appropriate action.
- Normative traditions disagree. Their inclusion here illustrates possible ethical grounding and is not empirical validation or a prescription of the user's beliefs.
