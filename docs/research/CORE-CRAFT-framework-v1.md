# The CORE & CRAFT Personal Framework — Iteration 1

## A dual-category operating system for career execution, life foundations, and long-term leverage

**C.O.R.E. — Foundations & Capital** organizes career direction, emotional well-being, relationships, and financial security. **C.R.A.F.T. — Execution, Systems & Growth** organizes learning, physical recovery, tools, ethical grounding, and time. The categories are a navigation aid, not a scientific hierarchy: physical recovery and ethics are foundations too, and no pillar is merely fuel for productivity.

This revision preserves the original names and practical intent while separating research-supported guidance from personal experiments and moral commitments. The complete nine-pillar package has not been clinically or experimentally validated. Read the [iteration 1 evidence audit](CORE-CRAFT-evidence-audit-v1.md) for each original rule's verdict, source details, and limitations. Bracketed source IDs below link to that audit.

### How to use it

- Choose practices that fit current health, resources, obligations, beliefs, and access. The framework is a decision aid, not a score of personal worth.
- Treat suggested daily and Sunday rhythms as optional defaults. A hard week, a missed session, or necessary care work is not a failed week.
- Use measurable health and financial guidelines with the populations and limitations specified; treat other numbers and schedules as experiments, not universal requirements.
- Protect people and ethical commitments while seeking leverage. Do not use a productivity target to justify routinely sacrificing sleep, safety, finances, or relationships.

# Part 1: C.O.R.E. — Foundations & Capital

## C — Career

**Definition.** Direct professional effort toward meaningful outcomes and sustainable advancement. Exercise available autonomy, make contributions and changing expectations visible, and use evidence of work in career conversations. A career includes enabling work, collaboration, learning, and maintenance—not only shipped features or promotions. Opportunity, resources, and other people's decisions also shape advancement.

**Operating rule.** Protect feasible focus on an agreed outcome; make progress, blockers, and changes visible rather than letting moving goalposts erase completed work. Progress monitoring has evidence across varied goals, but no study cited here validates mandatory daily wins or guarantees promotion. [CA1](CORE-CRAFT-evidence-audit-v1.md#ca1) [CA3](CORE-CRAFT-evidence-audit-v1.md#ca3)

**Protocol.**

1. Name the outcome, intended beneficiary, and what would count as a meaningful next milestone. Clarify scope and decision-makers when they are unclear.
2. Reserve a focus block when feasible. A 90-minute sprint is one optional starting point, not a biological optimum; choose a shorter or longer block for the task, fatigue, and obligations. [CA2](CORE-CRAFT-evidence-audit-v1.md#ca2) [CA4](CORE-CRAFT-evidence-audit-v1.md#ca4)
3. At shutdown or another convenient time, record a completed step, useful learning, decision, or blocker and its next action. If goals change, note the earlier agreement and the change; discuss material changes with the person who can clarify them. A daily entry is a useful personal default, not a requirement to invent a win.
4. Preserve appropriate, non-confidential evidence of contributions and share relevant results or career goals with stakeholders when useful.

## O — Outlook & Emotional Health

**Definition.** Notice emotional experience and stressors, respond flexibly, recover psychologically, and seek support when needed. Well-being includes room for difficult emotions as well as positive experiences; it is not the permanent absence of anxiety, sadness, or anger.

**Operating rule.** Observe feelings without treating them as failures. Track general well-being separately from acute stressful events, and choose how to express emotion according to safety and context. The evidence on expressive suppression varies by culture and emotion; it does not imply that disclosure is always safe or that every negative feeling must be eliminated. [OH1](CORE-CRAFT-evidence-audit-v1.md#oh1) [OH2](CORE-CRAFT-evidence-audit-v1.md#oh2)

**Protocol.**

1. In the weekly review, note meaningful stressors, emotional responses, recovery opportunities, and anything that helped or made things harder. If useful, briefly note a stressful event and response when it happens; this is personal reflection, not a validated acute-stress score.
2. Optionally use the original PERMA-Profiler for **general** well-being. Its positive-emotion scale and separate negative-emotion scale are not an acute-stress measure. Keep the original wording and scoring, consult the authors' usage conditions, and interpret changes cautiously rather than treating them as diagnoses. [OH1](CORE-CRAFT-evidence-audit-v1.md#oh1)
3. At work shutdown, optionally list unresolved tasks, the next action, and where to return to them. A small bedtime-writing experiment offers indirect support, not a guarantee that a work shutdown list improves sleep; stop or adjust if it increases rumination. [OH3](CORE-CRAFT-evidence-audit-v1.md#oh3)
4. If distress persists, worsens, or interferes with daily life, seek qualified support. Severe distress or immediate safety concerns can require help sooner; do not wait for a weekly review. [OH4](CORE-CRAFT-evidence-audit-v1.md#oh4)

## R — Relationships & Connections

**Definition.** Cultivate safe, reciprocal, meaningful ties with family, chosen family, friends, peers, and communities. Connection includes belonging, listening, giving and receiving support, shared experience, and the boundaries that make relationships sustainable. Solitude and differing social needs are legitimate.

**Operating rule.** Make room for relationships that are not merely transactional, while valuing care and honest conversations even when they are demanding rather than immediately energizing. Stronger social relationships are associated with health in observational research, but the evidence does not prescribe a specific number of contacts. [RC1](CORE-CRAFT-evidence-audit-v1.md#rc1) [RC2](CORE-CRAFT-evidence-audit-v1.md#rc2) [RC3](CORE-CRAFT-evidence-audit-v1.md#rc3)

**Protocol.**

1. Notice where connection feels supportive, where mutual care is needed, and where a boundary or more distance would be safer.
2. When desired, arrange a check-in or shared activity with someone who welcomes it; a call, message, or in-person interaction may count. Respect both people's time and capacity.
3. Two meaningful interactions per week can be a personal reminder if useful, **not** a validated health threshold or pass/fail quota. A caring but difficult interaction can still matter.

## E — Economic Security & Finances

**Definition.** Maintain an understandable picture of cash flow, essential obligations, reserves, debt, and—when appropriate—long-term investments. Build resilience against shocks and reduce avoidable administrative load without assuming an automated system can remove structural financial insecurity.

**Operating rule.** Cover essential obligations and preserve appropriate liquidity first; automate affordable, reversible routines and monitor them. Savings defaults can help, but balances, job changes, fees, withdrawals, and opt-outs matter. [EF1](CORE-CRAFT-evidence-audit-v1.md#ef1) [EF2](CORE-CRAFT-evidence-audit-v1.md#ef2)

**Protocol.**

1. Map income dates, essential expenses, debts, and due dates. If money is insufficient, consider the consequences of missed bills, prioritize urgent necessities and obligations, and contact creditors or an appropriate counselor; do not assume a budget can create missing income. [EF6](CORE-CRAFT-evidence-audit-v1.md#ef6)
2. Set a situation-appropriate emergency-reserve goal. If feasible, schedule a modest transfer when funds are available; use balance alerts and change or pause it if it risks an overdraft. [EF1](CORE-CRAFT-evidence-audit-v1.md#ef1)
3. If investing is appropriate, select and review an asset allocation in light of goals, time horizon, risk tolerance, fees, and taxes. Diversification does not guarantee against losses; no allocation is prescribed here. [EF3](CORE-CRAFT-evidence-audit-v1.md#ef3)
4. During the weekly pulse, check near-term bills, balances, alerts, and failed transfers. Review longer-term allocations at an appropriate less-frequent cadence and after material life changes. Automation reduces routine decisions; it does not eliminate oversight or guarantee security.

# Part 2: C.R.A.F.T. — Execution, Systems & Growth

## C — Competence & Mastery

**Definition.** Develop knowledge and judgment through intentional study, realistic practice, feedback, and later application. Expertise depends on the domain, opportunities, experience, and more than practice hours. Career execution delivers today's work; competence development improves what you can understand and do over time.

**Operating rule.** Protect learning from being entirely displaced by reactive tickets, while recognizing that a work task can also teach a skill when approached deliberately. Practice and feedback matter, but no universal dose or guaranteed path to mastery follows from the evidence. [CM3](CORE-CRAFT-evidence-audit-v1.md#cm3)

**Protocol.**

1. Pick a skill, question, paper, architecture decision, or model with relevance beyond an immediate ticket. Reserve a recurring study opportunity when feasible; weekly is a scheduling preference, not a validated minimum.
2. Study, then actively apply or explain the idea: solve a problem, sketch a model, or reconstruct a trade-off without looking. Compare with references, tests, or knowledgeable feedback.
3. Revisit important material after an interval and apply it in a realistic task. Retrieval and distributed practice have strong educational evidence, but vocabulary or classroom results do not guarantee transfer to engineering judgment. [CM1](CORE-CRAFT-evidence-audit-v1.md#cm1) [CM2](CORE-CRAFT-evidence-audit-v1.md#cm2)

## R — Recovery & Physical Health

**Definition.** Maintain the physical capacity for a good life through sufficient sleep, accessible movement, nourishment, medical care when needed, and environments that make recovery easier. Rest is valuable in itself, not merely a productivity input; disability, illness, care responsibilities, and access shape what is realistic.

**Operating rule.** Protect sleep opportunity and regular movement as infrastructure, not the routine buffer for work overflow. Adapt the details to health, circumstances, and professional advice rather than making an absolute rule that fails during illness or emergencies. [RH1](CORE-CRAFT-evidence-audit-v1.md#rh1) [RH2](CORE-CRAFT-evidence-audit-v1.md#rh2)

**Protocol.**

1. Protect a consistent sleep window where possible. CDC guidance for adults aged 18–60 recommends at least seven hours of sleep; age, quality, conditions, and individual needs matter. Discuss persistent sleep problems with a healthcare professional. [RH1](CORE-CRAFT-evidence-audit-v1.md#rh1)
2. Choose accessible movement. General CDC adult guidance is 150 minutes of moderate activity per week or an equivalent amount of vigorous activity, plus muscle strengthening on at least two days; some activity is better than none. Adjust for ability, condition, and clinical guidance rather than requiring a daily workout. [RH2](CORE-CRAFT-evidence-audit-v1.md#rh2)
3. Choose adequate, balanced, varied food as resources and health needs allow; there is no single prescribed menu. [RH3](CORE-CRAFT-evidence-audit-v1.md#rh3)
4. Try shutting down the development environment, moving devices away from bed, or staging activity gear. CDC recommends turning electronic devices off before bed; the specific gear and shutdown setup are personal experiments, not proven guarantees. Repetition and cues may help, but habit formation varies substantially. [RH1](CORE-CRAFT-evidence-audit-v1.md#rh1) [RH4](CORE-CRAFT-evidence-audit-v1.md#rh4) [RH5](CORE-CRAFT-evidence-audit-v1.md#rh5) [RH6](CORE-CRAFT-evidence-audit-v1.md#rh6)

## A — Agents & Systems

**Definition.** Design and maintain workflows, toolchains, and appropriate automation that reduce verified operational friction while preserving quality, security, privacy, and human accountability. This includes conventional deterministic automation, AI assistance, and tool-using AI agents; they are not interchangeable.

**Operating rule.** Delegate bounded, verifiable work only when the net benefit justifies setup, review, errors, and risk. Research on an assisted customer-support workflow and a developer coding trial produced different results in different settings; neither proves autonomous agents maximize leverage. [AS1](CORE-CRAFT-evidence-audit-v1.md#as1) [AS2](CORE-CRAFT-evidence-audit-v1.md#as2)

**Protocol.**

1. Select a repetitive task and describe its current workflow, expected output, quality requirements, and acceptable risks. Prefer a simpler script or human process if it is sufficient.
2. Limit access and sensitive data to what is needed. Treat external content as untrusted; AI agents can be redirected by malicious instructions in retrieved material. Require human approval before consequential or irreversible actions. [AS3](CORE-CRAFT-evidence-audit-v1.md#as3) [AS4](CORE-CRAFT-evidence-audit-v1.md#as4)
3. Trial the workflow on a bounded task. Compare with the prior approach using total human effort and elapsed time, including setup, supervision, verification, and rework; check output quality and incidents as well as speed.
4. Keep, modify, or stop the workflow based on observed results. Human review can also fail, so inspect whether the checks actually catch mistakes rather than assuming supervision guarantees safety.

## F — Faith & Ethics

**Definition.** Ground choices and long-term purpose in religious, moral, or philosophical commitments deliberately held, then apply those commitments to real situations with judgment and accountability. The person determines the tradition and principles; neither the research audit nor this framework prescribes a religion or empirically proves a moral doctrine.

**Operating rule.** Let chosen principles guide ambition, technical output, relationships, and ordinary decisions; remain willing to recognize conflicts, seek counsel, change course, and make repairs. Practical judgment requires attention to particulars as well as general principles. [FE1](CORE-CRAFT-evidence-audit-v1.md#fe1) [FE2](CORE-CRAFT-evidence-audit-v1.md#fe2)

**Protocol.**

1. Name the commitments that matter and their grounding—for example, a faith tradition, philosophical view, or both. The audit includes illustrative sources, not a required creed. [FE3](CORE-CRAFT-evidence-audit-v1.md#fe3) [FE4](CORE-CRAFT-evidence-audit-v1.md#fe4)
2. For a consequential decision, identify affected people, duties, foreseeable benefits and harms, and tensions among values. Consider who has a voice and whose interests might be overlooked.
3. Choose a defensible action; consult a trusted person or relevant tradition when useful. Revisit and repair the choice when new information or harm emerges. A checklist supports accountability but cannot guarantee integrity.

## T — Time Management & Planning

**Definition.** Allocate attention, calendar time, transitions, and recovery in a way that reflects priorities, actual constraints, and changing energy. Time management is the coordination layer for the other pillars, not a demand to fill every available hour.

**Operating rule.** Try demanding work at times you tend to feel alert and leave feasible recovery or transition space. Treat preferred hours and block lengths as hypotheses: chronotype effects vary by task and person; micro-break research supports vigor and fatigue outcomes more consistently than overall performance. [TM1](CORE-CRAFT-evidence-audit-v1.md#tm1) [TM2](CORE-CRAFT-evidence-audit-v1.md#tm2)

**Protocol.**

1. Put essential obligations, sleep opportunity, and important commitments on the calendar; reserve feasible blocks for focus, learning, relationships, and recovery without pretending they are all equally available every day.
2. Try a focus length suited to the task and leave transition or break time when possible. Ninety minutes may be a useful experiment, but neither a universal 90-minute cycle nor a fixed buffer duration is established. [TM3](CORE-CRAFT-evidence-audit-v1.md#tm3) [TM1](CORE-CRAFT-evidence-audit-v1.md#tm1)
3. Compare how work and fatigue vary across occasions. Consider sleep, task difficulty, interruptions, and obligations before attributing a result to a biological peak; adjust the calendar without sacrificing sleep.

# Weekly C.R.A.F.T. & C.O.R.E. Pulse Check

Use Sunday if convenient, or another day that fits. This is a reflection and adjustment tool, not a validated questionnaire, diagnostic instrument, composite score, or requirement to make progress in every dimension each week. Notice constraints; choose an action only where useful.

## C.O.R.E. audit

- **[C] Career:** What meaningful outcome, enabling work, learning, or blocker emerged? What contribution or changed expectation should I record, clarify, or share?
- **[O] Outlook & Emotional Health:** What stressors and emotions arose, what helped me respond or recover, and is there persistent distress for which I want support?
- **[R] Relationships & Connections:** Did I have the safe, meaningful connection or solitude I needed? Is there someone to check in with, support, or set a boundary with?
- **[E] Economic Security & Finances:** Are near-term essentials, balances, alerts, and automated transfers in order? Is an adjustment or outside assistance needed?

## C.R.A.F.T. audit

- **[C] Competence & Mastery:** What did I intentionally study or practice, what feedback exposed a gap, and what should I revisit or apply?
- **[R] Recovery & Physical Health:** How were my sleep opportunity and quality, movement, nourishment, and recovery environment? What realistic adjustment would help?
- **[A] Agents & Systems:** Compared with the prior workflow, did automation save effort after setup, review, and rework without unacceptable quality or security costs? Keep, change, or stop?
- **[F] Faith & Ethics:** Which decision tested a core commitment, who was affected, and is any clarification, change, or repair needed?
- **[T] Time Management & Planning:** When did focused work and recovery fit or conflict with my energy and obligations? What one feasible calendar adjustment is worth trying?

**Safety and scope.** This framework is educational and does not replace individualized medical, mental-health, financial, legal, or spiritual counsel. Seek urgent local help for an immediate safety threat; contact an appropriate professional for persistent health concerns, serious financial difficulty, or decisions beyond your expertise.
