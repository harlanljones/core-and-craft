# CORE & CRAFT: Evidence Audit — Iteration 2

**Research checked:** 29 October 2026. **Companion:** [Iteration 2 framework](CORE-CRAFT-framework-v2.md). **Previous round:** [Iteration 1 framework](CORE-CRAFT-framework-v1.md) and [original-claim audit](CORE-CRAFT-evidence-audit-v1.md).

## What this round audits

This targeted second-round audit checks each iteration 1 pillar's definition, operating rule, numbered protocol steps, and weekly pulse. The first audit maps those elements back to the user's original rules, detailed protocols, and questions; that mapping remains part of the record. This document adds source checks, new evidence, and explicit retain/qualify/extend decisions. It is not a comprehensive systematic review. Sources were prioritized by relevance and risk, especially for health, finance, measurement, and AI agents. At least three distinct sources are listed for each pillar; **new** means not listed for that pillar in the first audit, not necessarily newly published.

Linked source pages, abstracts, or available full texts were inspected. Bibliographic entries state what was inspected and important limitations. Evidence from an abstract alone is not a full risk-of-bias assessment. Associations are not automatically causal; study populations, tasks, interventions, and controls matter. Professional codes and religious/philosophical texts express normative commitments, not scientific proofs. The combined framework, its categories, and its pulse check have not been validated as one intervention.

**Evidence verdicts:** **Supported** means a narrower claim has relevant evidence or authoritative guidance; **mixed** means support is partial, indirect, context-dependent, or does not establish the full prescription; **unsupported** means a specific claim is not established by the sources reviewed, not that it is proven false; **values-based** identifies a preference, goal, or normative commitment that research alone cannot settle. **Retain**, **qualify**, and **extend** describe the editing decision, not evidence strength. Personal routines, including daily logs, weekly reviews, 90-minute focus blocks, and two social interactions, are not universal validated doses.

## Key changes from iteration 1

1. Separate general well-being, last-month perceived stress, and an event-level personal observation; add optional support and practical stress responses without turning any score into a diagnosis.
2. Add resumption cues and escalation routes for context switches; strengthen technical learning by testing whether an idea transfers to a different case.
3. Make reserve-versus-debt tradeoffs explicit instead of implying one rigid financial sequence; distinguish a descriptive hypothetical experiment from optimal financial advice.
4. Distinguish sleep hygiene from treatment for chronic insomnia; preserve clinical assessment and guideline-backed options.
5. Update the historical 2025 developer-AI result with METR's qualified 2026 follow-up; enforce agent permissions and high-impact approval outside the model, and measure costs and failures separately.
6. Add professional-computing responsibilities and distinguish ethical lenses without claiming a checklist resolves moral disagreement.

### Sources — Career

- <a id="ca1"></a> **CA1 — retained.** Harkin et al. (2016), *Does Monitoring Goal Progress Promote Goal Attainment?* [University repository abstract](https://eprints.whiterose.ac.uk/id/eprint/87431/). Peer-reviewed meta-analysis of 138 randomized studies found monitoring interventions improved goal attainment on average. Goals and interventions varied; this is not proof of a daily career log or promotion.
- <a id="ca2"></a> **CA2 — retained.** Humphrey, Nahrgang & Morgeson (2007), *Integrating Motivational, Social, and Contextual Work Design Features*. [Full article](http://www.morgeson.com/downloads/humphrey_nahrgang_morgeson_2007.pdf). Meta-analysis of work-design correlations across 259 studies distinguished method, scheduling and decision autonomy along with social/contextual features. Associations do not establish an individually achievable autonomy intervention or a focus duration.
- <a id="ca3"></a> **CA3 — new.** DeLine & Parnin (2010), *Evaluating Cues for Resuming Interrupted Programming Tasks*. [Institutional abstract](https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/research/publication/evaluating-cues-for-resuming-interrupted-programming-tasks/). A survey of 371 programmers and a laboratory comparison examined resumption; automated cues outperformed notes alone in the lab. This does not prove a handwritten note is superior to no cue or guarantee gains in every workplace.
- <a id="ca5"></a> **CA5 — retained.** Nobel (2011), *How Small Wins Unleash Creativity*. [Harvard Business School article](https://www.library.hbs.edu/working-knowledge/how-small-wins-unleash-creativity). Reports Amabile and Kramer's work-diary research and interviews, including examples of shifting goals and resource constraints. Observed associations and interpretations do not establish that logging causes career advancement.

### Sources — Outlook & Emotional Health

- <a id="oh1"></a> **OH1 — retained.** Butler & Kern (2016), *The PERMA-Profiler: A brief multidimensional measure of flourishing*. [Original article](https://www.internationaljournalofwellbeing.org/index.php/ijow/article/view/526); [authors' instrument and scoring](https://www.peggykern.org/uploads/5/6/6/7/56678211/the_perma-profiler_101416.pdf). Measure-development and validation studies assess general well-being; positive and negative emotion are separate scales. The 16-item overall score includes the happiness item, not negative emotion. General wording and short subscales limit event-specific interpretation; follow use conditions. Article and instructions count as one source here.
- <a id="oh2"></a> **OH2 — new.** Patterson, Sagui-Henson & Prather (2020), *Measures of Psychosocial Stress and Stressful Exposures*. [Full review](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8063511/). Describes PSS-10 as a last-month self-report of perceived stress, not a diagnostic instrument; populations and translations require attention. The review's stated use conditions are older than the current author site, so consult [OH7](#oh7) for permissions.
- <a id="oh3"></a> **OH3 — retained.** Fernandes & Tone (2021), *A systematic review and meta-analysis of the association between expressive suppression and positive affect*. [PubMed abstract](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/34325115/). Across 41 effect sizes, the overall association was negative, but associations varied by culture and emotion suppressed. The review assesses expressive behavior and associations, not a causal requirement to express every emotion.
- <a id="oh4"></a> **OH4 — new.** World Health Organization (2020), *Doing What Matters in Times of Stress*. [Institutional guidance](https://www.who.int/publications/i/item/9789240003927). Offers evidence-informed, field-tested self-help skills; the guidance is not a trial of every exercise or a substitute for care.
- <a id="oh5"></a> **OH5 — retained.** Scullin et al. (2018), *The Effects of Bedtime Writing on Difficulty Falling Asleep*. [PubMed abstract](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/29058942/). Fifty-seven healthy young adults were randomized to two types of writing before one laboratory night; the future-task group fell asleep faster. No no-writing control or chronic-insomnia sample; work-shutdown application is indirect.
- <a id="oh6"></a> **OH6 — retained.** National Institute of Mental Health (reviewed 2026), *Caring for Your Mental Health*. [Institutional guidance](https://www.nimh.nih.gov/health/topics/caring-for-your-mental-health). Suggests individualized self-care, professional support and urgent crisis resources; its examples are guidance rather than validation of this framework. Help may be sought before a symptom-duration threshold.
- <a id="oh7"></a> **OH7 — new.** Carnegie Mellon University, Cohen laboratory, *Scales: Perceived Stress Scale*. [Instrument and current permission information](https://www.cmu.edu/dietrich/psychology/stress-immunity-disease-lab/scales/index.html). The authors' site states that PSS is not diagnostic and has no score cutoffs and directs permission requests through the named platform. Translation and use terms vary; check current instructions before use.

### Sources — Relationships & Connections

- <a id="rc1"></a> **RC1 — retained.** Holt-Lunstad, Smith & Layton (2010), *Social Relationships and Mortality Risk*. [PubMed abstract](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/20668659/). Meta-analysis of 148 prospective studies found stronger relationships associated with survival; observational evidence and varied measures do not establish causality or a contact quota.
- <a id="rc2"></a> **RC2 — retained.** Lincoln (2000), *Social Support, Negative Social Interactions, and Psychological Well-Being*. [Full review](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4651456/). Critical review of 28 studies describes positive and negative interactions and inconsistent results; it cannot establish that negative interactions always outweigh support.
- <a id="rc3"></a> **RC3 — new.** Lasgaard et al. (2026), *Are loneliness interventions effective for reducing loneliness?* [PubMed abstract](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/41129341/). Meta-analysis reported short-term benefit across randomized trials, but graded certainty low or very low and could not determine who benefits most. Interventions are heterogeneous; effects are not evidence for a specific informal contact quota.
- <a id="rc4"></a> **RC4 — new.** Blodgett et al. (2025), *What works to reduce loneliness: a rapid systematic review of 101 interventions*. [Full review](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12119363/). Defines loneliness as an unwanted mismatch in desired connection, distinct from isolation; reviews varied interventions. Many studies lacked controls or randomization, and OECD/English-language restrictions limit generalizability.

### Sources — Time Management & Planning

- <a id="tm1"></a> **TM1 — retained.** Albulescu et al. (2022), *“Give me a break!”* [PubMed abstract](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/36044424/). Meta-analysis of experimental and quasi-experimental samples found average improvements in vigor and fatigue, but no statistically significant overall performance effect. No universal break length follows.
- <a id="tm2"></a> **TM2 — retained.** Chauhan et al. (2025), *Chronotype and synchrony effects in human cognitive performance*. [PubMed abstract](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/40293205/). Systematic review found synchrony effects in some studies, varying by task, age and conditions; subjective preference does not establish a fixed individual peak.
- <a id="tm3"></a> **TM3 — new.** Guo et al. (2021), *Effects of interventions to reduce the negative consequences of interruptions on task performance*. [PubMed abstract](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/34273814/). Review of 33 laboratory experiments found intervention effects on primary-task accuracy and resumption lag that varied by intervention and task. It does not validate a particular note or fixed cost in field engineering work.
- <a id="tm4"></a> **TM4 — retained.** Neubauer & Freudenthaler (1995), *Ultradian rhythms in cognitive performance: no evidence for a 1.5-h rhythm*. [PubMed abstract](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/7669837/). In 60 people performing one task, a conservative analysis found no significant 90-minute periodicity; a null in this setting is not proof that 90-minute blocks never help.
- <a id="tm5"></a> **TM5 — retained.** Hayashi, Sato & Hori (1994), *Ultradian rhythms in task performance, self-evaluation, and EEG activity*. [PubMed abstract](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/7870505/). Ten male students showed a behavioral/subjective fluctuation at 12 cycles per day (120 minutes) and different EEG rhythms. The study did not test competing work schedules or establish a universal 90-minute optimum.

## Evidence boundaries and unresolved questions

- No reviewed study validates the combined CORE & CRAFT framework, its category structure, weekly checklist, or a composite score.
- A PSS-10 score is past-month perceived stress, not an acute event or diagnosis; PERMA reflects general well-being. No individual clinical thresholds are supplied here.
- The 2026 loneliness-intervention evidence is encouraging but low or very low certainty; its results do not establish a universal format, frequency, or effect for an informal conversation.
- Studies of learning and interruptions often use students or laboratory tasks; an individual's results and transfer to professional engineering remain uncertain.
- AI research is task-, tool-, population-, and time-specific. The 2026 METR update explicitly warns that selection and measurement make its current productivity estimate unreliable; it is not evidence that all agents help or hurt.
- Financial and clinical guidance is general, often U.S.-specific, and depends on local law, health, access and personal circumstances. The debt experiment was hypothetical and cannot decide a person's allocation.
- Religious and philosophical sources provide reasons and commitments within different traditions; they do not scientifically establish faith or automatically resolve conflicts between moral duties and consequences.
