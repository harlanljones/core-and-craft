# The CORE & CRAFT Personal Framework — Iteration 3

## A dual-category framework for life foundations, career execution, and long-term growth

**C.O.R.E. — Foundations & Capital** organizes career direction, emotional well-being, relationships, and financial security. **C.R.A.F.T. — Execution, Systems & Growth** organizes learning, physical recovery, tools, ethical grounding, and time. The acronym and all nine pillar names are unchanged. These categories are a navigation aid, not a scientific hierarchy: recovery and ethics are foundations too, and no person's worth depends on productivity.

Iteration 3 expands the definitions and adds practical distinctions about work authority and resources, social connection, financial resilience, learning calibration, exertion safety, agent outputs, and ethical care. The full nine-pillar package has **not** been validated as a combined intervention. Bracketed source IDs link to the [iteration 3 evidence audit](CORE-CRAFT-evidence-audit-v3.md), which assesses the previous definition, rule, protocol steps, and pulse for each pillar and describes study limitations. Earlier versions remain available: [iteration 2 framework](CORE-CRAFT-framework-v2.md), [iteration 2 audit](CORE-CRAFT-evidence-audit-v2.md), [iteration 1 framework](CORE-CRAFT-framework-v1.md), and [original-claim audit](CORE-CRAFT-evidence-audit-v1.md).

### How to use it

- Select practices that fit your health, resources, obligations, beliefs, safety, and access. Adapt or skip the rest. A missed practice, necessary care work, or a difficult week is information, not failure.
- A daily note, weekly review, two-interaction reminder, or 90-minute focus block is an optional personal choice, **not** a validated dose or pass/fail test. Population guidance has its own scope and is not an entry requirement.
- Attend to urgent health, safety, financial, or ethical concerns when they arise; do not wait for a pulse check. A plan cannot guarantee that resources, authority, care, or safe choices are available.
- Keep notes private and proportionate. If tracking or writing increases distress, rumination, or privacy risk, stop or change the practice. The framework is educational and cannot replace individualized medical, mental-health, financial, legal, or spiritual counsel.
- When priorities conflict, name the actual tradeoff and identify what is under your control. For example, a deadline that would routinely displace sleep may call for a scope, staffing, or timing decision—not simply a longer workday. Seek a safer adjustment or support where feasible; success also depends on other people and conditions.

# Part 1: C.O.R.E. — Foundations & Capital

## C — Career

**Definition.** Direct professional effort toward meaningful outcomes and sustainable advancement. Career capital includes practical skills, working relationships, trustworthy evidence of contributions, and opportunities to exercise judgment. It encompasses maintenance, enabling work, collaboration, learning, and care—not only shipped features or promotions. Autonomy over methods, timing, or decisions is distinct from having the authority, time, information, staffing, or budget to act. Opportunity and other people's decisions also shape advancement. [CA2](CORE-CRAFT-evidence-audit-v3.md#ca2) [CA4](CORE-CRAFT-evidence-audit-v3.md#ca4)

**Operating rule.** Protect feasible attention for an agreed outcome; make progress, blockers, and changes visible without mistaking activity or manufactured wins for impact. Progress monitoring has evidence across varied goals, but does not by itself supply resources, resolve changing priorities, or guarantee recognition. [CA1](CORE-CRAFT-evidence-audit-v3.md#ca1) [CA5](CORE-CRAFT-evidence-audit-v3.md#ca5)

**Protocol.**

1. **Agree on the work.** Name the outcome and beneficiary, the next meaningful milestone, what success looks like, and who can decide scope or resolve a blocker. Clarify decision rights, resources, constraints, and feedback when possible. If demands exceed capacity, identify the needed tradeoff or resource and, where safe, ask the person with authority to decide or reprioritize. A request is not a guarantee that support will be available.
2. **Create feasible focus.** Reserve a task-suited block when the work allows; agree on an urgent-contact route and pause for fatigue or necessary obligations. Ninety minutes is one optional experiment, not a biological optimum. After an interruption, a brief note or tool-generated context cue may help you resume; choose what works locally. [CA3](CORE-CRAFT-evidence-audit-v3.md#ca3) [TM4](CORE-CRAFT-evidence-audit-v3.md#tm4)
3. **Make the record useful.** At shutdown or another convenient time, optionally record a completed step, learning, decision, or blocker and its next action. A short daily entry can be useful; no win needs to be invented. If an agreement changes, note the earlier expectation and the change, then seek clarification from the person able to resolve it.
4. **Communicate responsibly.** Preserve permitted, non-confidential evidence of contributions; share relevant results, career goals, or requests for feedback when useful. Credit collaborators fairly. Visibility is not a substitute for organizational support.

## O — Outlook & Emotional Health

**Definition.** Notice emotional experiences and sources of stress, respond in ways suited to the situation, recover psychologically, and seek support when useful. Emotional health includes positive and difficult feelings, daily functioning, relationships, material circumstances, and access to support. It is not a demand for constant positivity, disclosure, or recovery on a schedule. How and whether to express an emotion depends on culture, context, and safety. [OH3](CORE-CRAFT-evidence-audit-v3.md#oh3)

**Operating rule.** Treat emotions as information, not personal failures or infallible instructions. Separate an event from general well-being and from perceived stress over a longer period. Observation should serve understanding or action; individual coping cannot by itself remove every unsafe or materially difficult condition.

**Protocol.**

1. **Notice and respond if useful.** Consider a stressor, your response, its effect on functioning, and what helped or made recovery harder. A next step might be a rest or grounding break, changing a demand, requesting an accommodation, seeking material help, or contacting support. Brief event notes or ratings are informal observations, not validated acute-stress scores. Structured support interventions have limited, low-certainty evidence; an informal conversation is not the same as a tested treatment. [OH4](CORE-CRAFT-evidence-audit-v3.md#oh4) [OH8](CORE-CRAFT-evidence-audit-v3.md#oh8)
2. **Choose measurement only if useful.** The optional PERMA-Profiler concerns **general well-being**: positive and negative emotion are separate scales, and neither is an acute-stress measure. The optional PSS-10 asks about perceived stress over the **past month**, not one event; weekly administrations have overlapping recall periods. Use the original wording and scoring and check current permissions. Neither instrument diagnoses a disorder or supplies a clinical cutoff. The PERMA authors' overall score averages the 15 PERMA items plus the separate happiness item; it does not subtract negative emotion. [OH1](CORE-CRAFT-evidence-audit-v3.md#oh1) [OH2](CORE-CRAFT-evidence-audit-v3.md#oh2) [OH7](CORE-CRAFT-evidence-audit-v3.md#oh7)
3. **Close open loops if it helps.** At shutdown, optionally list unresolved work, the next action, and where to return. A small bedtime-writing study compared two kinds of writing rather than a work-shutdown routine; this is a personal experiment, not an insomnia treatment. Stop or adapt if it increases rumination. [OH5](CORE-CRAFT-evidence-audit-v3.md#oh5)
4. **Get support without a prerequisite.** Seek help when concerned, including before symptoms become persistent or impairing; no score or waiting period is required. A qualified professional can assess distress that persists, worsens, or interferes with life. For immediate danger or urgent safety concerns, contact appropriate local emergency or crisis services rather than waiting for a review. [OH6](CORE-CRAFT-evidence-audit-v3.md#oh6)

## R — Relationships & Connections

**Definition.** Cultivate safe, meaningful ties with family, chosen family, friends, peers, and communities, while respecting boundaries and individual needs for solitude. Connection has several dimensions: **structure**—the roles, ties, and contact available; **function**—the emotional or practical support exchanged; and **quality**—whether interactions are respectful, satisfying, strained, or unsafe. Reciprocity need not be immediate or identical, particularly in caregiving. Loneliness is a distressing gap between desired and actual connection; social isolation concerns limited social roles, relationships, or contact. Chosen solitude is not the same as loneliness. [RC2](CORE-CRAFT-evidence-audit-v3.md#rc2) [RC5](CORE-CRAFT-evidence-audit-v3.md#rc5)

**Operating rule.** Make room for connection that is not merely transactional, without assuming every worthwhile interaction feels energizing or every relationship is beneficial. Prioritize mutual respect and safety. Observational research associates stronger social relationships with health; it does not establish a particular contact count or prove that any specific tie causes an outcome. [RC1](CORE-CRAFT-evidence-audit-v3.md#rc1) [RC2](CORE-CRAFT-evidence-audit-v3.md#rc2)

**Protocol.**

1. **Notice quality and safety.** Consider where connection provides care, where mutual support is needed, and where more distance or a boundary would help. Direct confrontation is not always safe; trusted outside help, confidential support, or distance may be preferable where available.
2. **Make connection accessible.** If desired, arrange a mutually welcome check-in, shared activity, or exchange of support by message, call, in person, or another accessible format. Consider community resources or qualified support when existing ties do not meet your needs. Transportation, cost, disability access, discrimination, capacity, and safety can limit options. Structured loneliness interventions show promising average results, but certainty is low or very low; a casual check-in is not equivalent to a tested treatment. [RC3](CORE-CRAFT-evidence-audit-v3.md#rc3) [RC5](CORE-CRAFT-evidence-audit-v3.md#rc5)
3. **Use reminders without quotas.** Two meaningful interactions in a week can be an optional reminder if useful—not a health threshold, target, or score of relationship quality. Difficult, caring conversations can matter; so can solitude. Nobody is required to reconnect with an unsafe person.

## E — Economic Security & Finances

**Definition.** Understand income, cash flow, essential obligations, debt, accessible reserves, and—when appropriate—long-term assets. Economic security includes the capacity to meet commitments, absorb shocks, and make meaningful choices; perceived control and actual resources are related but not identical. An automated account or larger balance alone cannot eliminate inadequate income, unequal access, or structural insecurity. [EF2](CORE-CRAFT-evidence-audit-v3.md#ef2) [EF7](CORE-CRAFT-evidence-audit-v3.md#ef7)

**Operating rule.** Attend to essentials and legal obligations while balancing accessible cash, borrowing costs, benefits, and longer-term goals. Automate only what is affordable and monitor it. There is no universal ordering or percentage for a reserve, expensive debt, and investing; a budget cannot create missing income. [EF1](CORE-CRAFT-evidence-audit-v3.md#ef1) [EF9](CORE-CRAFT-evidence-audit-v3.md#ef9)

**Protocol.**

1. **Map obligations and timing.** Note income dates, essential expenses, due dates, debt minimums and interest rates, and likely shortfalls. If you may be unable to pay, consider the consequences for housing, income, insurance, and legal obligations; contact creditors early where possible and ask about available arrangements. Check terms and any counseling fees before agreeing. Be cautious of promises to erase debt or instructions to stop communicating with a creditor. Availability and law depend on jurisdiction. [EF6](CORE-CRAFT-evidence-audit-v3.md#ef6)
2. **Balance resilience and debt.** Choose an accessible emergency reserve appropriate to your circumstances, even if starting small is all that is feasible. Weigh a buffer against high-interest debt, required payments, employer benefits, and other obligations rather than applying a fixed sequence. If feasible, schedule a modest transfer when funds are available, use balance alerts, and pause or change it to avoid overdrafts. A reserve is there to use during a shock and rebuild when possible; using it is not failure. Studies of particular savings offers show varied outcomes and do not determine your optimal split. [EF1](CORE-CRAFT-evidence-audit-v3.md#ef1) [EF3](CORE-CRAFT-evidence-audit-v3.md#ef3) [EF4](CORE-CRAFT-evidence-audit-v3.md#ef4)
3. **Consider investing when appropriate.** Before selecting an allocation, consider goals, time horizon, risk tolerance, debt costs, any employer match, taxes, and access to funds. For money needed soon, consider both accessibility and risk of loss. For investments, compare ongoing, transaction, and relevant exit or transfer fees as well as suitability; the lowest fee alone does not make a product appropriate. Diversification does not eliminate loss. No security, allocation, or personal ordering is prescribed here. [EF5](CORE-CRAFT-evidence-audit-v3.md#ef5) [EF8](CORE-CRAFT-evidence-audit-v3.md#ef8)
4. **Review the whole picture.** Check upcoming bills, balances, alerts, and failed transfers on a cadence that fits pay and obligations; review longer-term investments as appropriate and after material changes. Consider debt and the ability to meet expenses alongside savings: a higher balance in one account can reflect a transfer or borrowing rather than improved overall security. Seek qualified help when a tradeoff exceeds your expertise. [EF2](CORE-CRAFT-evidence-audit-v3.md#ef2)

# Part 2: C.R.A.F.T. — Execution, Systems & Growth

## C — Competence & Mastery

**Definition.** Develop knowledge, technical skill, and judgment through intentional study, realistic practice, feedback, reflection, and application. Competence includes recognizing uncertainty, adapting to unfamiliar situations, and **calibration**: comparing confidence with evidence of actual performance and revising both confidence and practice. Expertise depends on domain, prior knowledge, opportunity, feedback, and more than practice hours. Career execution delivers current work; competence development increases what you can understand and do over time. [CM4](CORE-CRAFT-evidence-audit-v3.md#cm4) [CM5](CORE-CRAFT-evidence-audit-v3.md#cm5)

**Operating rule.** Protect intentional learning from being entirely displaced by reactive work, while recognizing that a supervised, feedback-rich work task can also teach. No universal study dose or automatic transfer from classroom exercises to professional judgment is established. [CM1](CORE-CRAFT-evidence-audit-v3.md#cm1) [CM2](CORE-CRAFT-evidence-audit-v3.md#cm2)

**Protocol.**

1. **Choose a target.** Pick a skill, question, paper, model, or architecture tradeoff with relevance beyond the immediate task. Name a representative task and what adequate performance would look like. Reserve a recurring learning opportunity when feasible; weekly is a scheduling preference, not a minimum.
2. **Study actively and calibrate.** Study, then explain or reconstruct an idea without looking, solve a representative problem, or practice in a realistic simulation. If useful, note your expected result or confidence before checking. Compare the attempt with reliable references, tests, or qualified feedback; identify mistakes and unwarranted confidence, then choose what to practice next. Use examples, scaffolding, and difficulty suited to prior knowledge and the task. A small classroom intervention supports feedback-based study in its setting, not a guarantee of workplace results. [CM3](CORE-CRAFT-evidence-audit-v3.md#cm3) [CM5](CORE-CRAFT-evidence-audit-v3.md#cm5)
3. **Revisit and test transfer.** Return to important material after an interval. Try a different representative case, identify where assumptions fail, and obtain appropriate supervision or safeguards before high-stakes use. Testing can improve transfer on average in studied educational tasks, but effects vary and local transfer must be checked. [CM2](CORE-CRAFT-evidence-audit-v3.md#cm2)

## R — Recovery & Physical Health

**Definition.** Support physical health and a good life through sleep, tolerable movement, nourishment, rest, symptom management, accommodations, and medical care when needed. Rest has value in itself, not merely as fuel for work. Disability, illness, medication, age, caregiving, resources, and access shape what is realistic; a health condition is not a personal failure.

**Operating rule.** Protect sleep opportunity and activity that is appropriate to your health, not the routine buffer for work overflow. Population guidance provides context rather than a personal performance test. Do not push through delayed or disproportionate worsening after exertion to meet a target. [RH1](CORE-CRAFT-evidence-audit-v3.md#rh1) [RH3](CORE-CRAFT-evidence-audit-v3.md#rh3) [RH7](CORE-CRAFT-evidence-audit-v3.md#rh7)

**Protocol.**

1. **Protect sleep and seek care when needed.** Aim for a consistent opportunity to sleep where possible. CDC guidance recommends at least seven hours of **actual sleep** for adults aged 18–60; needs and recommendations vary by age and health. Discuss persistent sleep difficulties with a healthcare professional. For adults diagnosed with chronic insomnia, the AASM strongly recommends multicomponent cognitive behavioral therapy for insomnia (CBT-I) and conditionally recommends against sleep hygiene as the sole treatment. Ask a clinician about suitable options; not every sleep problem is insomnia. [RH1](CORE-CRAFT-evidence-audit-v3.md#rh1) [RH2](CORE-CRAFT-evidence-audit-v3.md#rh2)
2. **Move in tolerable, accessible ways.** General CDC adult guidance describes 150 minutes of moderate or 75 minutes of vigorous activity per week, or an equivalent combination, plus muscle strengthening on at least two days; its general message is that some activity is better than none. These are not targets to force despite illness. **If physical or mental exertion causes delayed or disproportionate symptom worsening—such as post-exertional malaise in ME/CFS—do not push through or automatically increase activity.** Discuss individualized activity management and care with a clinician knowledgeable about the condition. Pacing is not presented as a cure. [RH3](CORE-CRAFT-evidence-audit-v3.md#rh3) [RH7](CORE-CRAFT-evidence-audit-v3.md#rh7)
3. **Nourish.** Seek adequate, safe, balanced, varied food as health needs, culture, preferences, and resources allow. Food assistance, accessible preparation, or a qualified clinician or dietitian may help when appropriate. There is no single prescribed menu or moral score for eating. [RH4](CORE-CRAFT-evidence-audit-v3.md#rh4)
4. **Make recovery easier.** If useful, try shutting down the development environment, changing device use before bed, or staging activity gear. Devices may be needed for safety, accessibility, communication, or care. These are optional environmental cues, not guaranteed treatments; habit formation varies and has no universal deadline. [RH5](CORE-CRAFT-evidence-audit-v3.md#rh5) [RH6](CORE-CRAFT-evidence-audit-v3.md#rh6)

## A — Agents & Systems

**Definition.** Design and maintain workflows and toolchains that reduce observed operational friction without surrendering quality, security, privacy, or human accountability. A deterministic script follows explicit rules; a conversational assistant suggests content for a person to assess; a tool-using agent can act through connected systems; orchestration coordinates tools or agents. Risk depends on the data and functions exposed as well as the system's label.

**Operating rule.** Delegate bounded, verifiable work only when the result is useful **and** risk is acceptable. Time saved is not permission to compromise safety, privacy, or obligations. A customer-support assistant and an early-2025 developer trial had different results in different settings; a 2026 follow-up warned that selection and concurrent-work measurement made its productivity estimate unreliable. None establishes the value of your current workflow. [AS1](CORE-CRAFT-evidence-audit-v3.md#as1) [AS2](CORE-CRAFT-evidence-audit-v3.md#as2) [AS3](CORE-CRAFT-evidence-audit-v3.md#as3)

**Protocol.**

1. **Define the job and baseline.** Describe the current process, representative task mix, intended output, quality criteria, responsible person, failure modes, and unacceptable harms. Identify required information sources and independent checks; a plausible citation or authentic document is not proof of a claim and can contain hostile instructions. Prefer a simpler human process or deterministic script when sufficient.
2. **Bound authority, data, and outputs.** Provide only necessary functions and a scoped identity, with read-only access when sufficient; enforce authorization in the downstream system, not only in a prompt. Minimize sensitive data and check approved destinations and retention. Treat retrieved pages, messages, tool results, and agent reports as untrusted **data**, not instructions. Validate tool arguments and handle generated output safely at its destination—for example, use context-appropriate encoding and parameterized database queries; do not automatically execute unreviewed generated code. Require an approval gate outside the agent before consequential or irreversible actions, with stop and, where feasible, rollback. Human review and controls can fail. [AS4](CORE-CRAFT-evidence-audit-v3.md#as4) [AS6](CORE-CRAFT-evidence-audit-v3.md#as6)
3. **Compare and test safely.** On reasonably comparable work, record setup, oversight, verification, rework, direct and recurring costs, output quality, incidents, and failed or abandoned tasks—not just successes. Track hands-on human time separately from elapsed wall time; concurrent waiting is not necessarily human labor. In authorized, controlled conditions, test relevant errors and adversarial inputs, including whether permission and approval controls work. Local comparisons inform a decision but do not establish a universal effect; a clean test does not prove absence of vulnerabilities. [AS3](CORE-CRAFT-evidence-audit-v3.md#as3) [AS7](CORE-CRAFT-evidence-audit-v3.md#as7)
4. **Keep, change, or stop.** Assess benefit and risk separately against agreed boundaries; do not trade an unacceptable incident for speed. Stop and address serious failures immediately. Reassess task, model, tools, data destinations, permissions, and relevant checks after material changes, and remove access no longer needed.

## F — Faith & Ethics

**Definition.** Ground choices and long-term purpose in religious faith, moral or philosophical commitments, professional responsibilities, and the relationships or communities that inform them; apply those commitments with judgment and accountability. Faith and ethics can be related but are not identical: a person may be religious, secular, questioning, or drawing on more than one tradition. This pillar does not prescribe a creed, require private disclosure, or treat scientific results as proof of a moral or religious doctrine. [FE5](CORE-CRAFT-evidence-audit-v3.md#fe5) [FE6](CORE-CRAFT-evidence-audit-v3.md#fe6) [FE7](CORE-CRAFT-evidence-audit-v3.md#fe7)

**Operating rule.** Let chosen commitments guide ambition, technical output, relationships, and ordinary decisions. Recognize genuine conflicts among obligations; listen to affected people where safe, seek appropriate counsel, change course, and repair harm when possible. Different ethical traditions provide lenses, not a mechanical decision algorithm. [FE1](CORE-CRAFT-evidence-audit-v3.md#fe1) [FE4](CORE-CRAFT-evidence-audit-v3.md#fe4)

**Protocol.**

1. **Identify commitments.** Name the principles and sources of guidance that matter, privately if preferred. Consider whether inherited, community, personal, and professional commitments agree or conflict; examining them does not require disclosing or adopting another person's beliefs. Law and professional responsibilities depend on role and jurisdiction.
2. **Examine a consequential choice.** Identify affected people and whose voice may be missing. Consider foreseeable benefits and harms; duties, consent, and respect for persons; character and practical judgment; and the needs, vulnerability, relationships, and burden of care involved. These lenses can disagree, and no checklist automatically resolves the conflict. In computing, consider accessibility, fairness, privacy, confidentiality, security, and professional responsibilities. [FE1](CORE-CRAFT-evidence-audit-v3.md#fe1) [FE2](CORE-CRAFT-evidence-audit-v3.md#fe2) [FE3](CORE-CRAFT-evidence-audit-v3.md#fe3) [FE4](CORE-CRAFT-evidence-audit-v3.md#fe4) [FE7](CORE-CRAFT-evidence-audit-v3.md#fe7)
3. **Act and remain accountable.** Choose a defensible action, consult affected people or trusted counsel where safe, and document important decisions proportionately. Where appropriate, explain the decision and offer a way to challenge it or seek redress; confidentiality and safety can limit disclosure. Redesign, escalate, or decline when warranted, and revise or repair if harm or new information emerges. Power and access can constrain available choices.

## T — Time Management & Planning

**Definition.** Structure, protect, and adapt attention, calendar time, obligations, transitions, interruptions, and recovery as conditions change. Planning coordinates the other pillars; it does not require filling every hour or assuming a fixed biological peak. Some interruptions are necessary, and scheduling freedom varies. [TM6](CORE-CRAFT-evidence-audit-v3.md#tm6)

**Operating rule.** Try demanding work at times you tend to feel alert, protect feasible recovery and transitions, and preserve an urgent-contact route. Treat preferred hours, focus lengths, and breaks as hypotheses rather than laws. Rest can be valuable even when no productivity improvement is measured. [TM1](CORE-CRAFT-evidence-audit-v3.md#tm1) [TM2](CORE-CRAFT-evidence-audit-v3.md#tm2)

**Protocol.**

1. **Place commitments realistically.** Calendar essential obligations, sleep opportunity, and important commitments where feasible. Leave room for unpredictable demands when possible, and adapt the plan if authority, care needs, or capacity change. There is no fixed required buffer or daily block for every pillar.
2. **Plan focus and re-entry.** Choose a block suited to the task and leave feasible transition or break time. Ninety minutes is an optional experiment, not a proven optimum; shorter or longer may fit better. Preserve a route for urgent contact, distinguish necessary from negotiable interruptions, and optionally leave a next action or other context cue before a planned switch. Interruption studies do not prove one cue works for everyone. [TM3](CORE-CRAFT-evidence-audit-v3.md#tm3) [TM4](CORE-CRAFT-evidence-audit-v3.md#tm4) [TM5](CORE-CRAFT-evidence-audit-v3.md#tm5)
3. **Observe and adjust.** Compare work, fatigue, and recovery over several occasions if useful. Consider sleep, task difficulty, interruptions, and obligations before attributing a difference to a biological rhythm. Adjust what is within your control without sacrificing sleep to chase a supposed peak; personal observation does not isolate a causal effect.

# Weekly C.R.A.F.T. & C.O.R.E. Pulse Check

Use Sunday or another convenient day. This is a menu for reflection and adjustment, **not** a validated questionnaire, composite score, or requirement to improve in every dimension each week. Skip questions that do not apply; choose one feasible action if useful. Respond to urgent concerns immediately rather than waiting for the review.

## C.O.R.E. audit

- **[C] Career:** What meaningful outcome, enabling work, learning, or blocker emerged? What should I record or share, and which changed expectations, decisions, or resource requests need clarification by someone with authority?
- **[O] Outlook & Emotional Health:** What stressors and feelings mattered, how did they affect functioning, and what recovery, practical help, accommodation, or support might be useful?
- **[R] Relationships & Connections:** Did I have the safe, accessible connection or solitude I wanted? Is there support, a community resource, or a boundary I want to consider?
- **[E] Economic Security & Finances:** Are upcoming essentials, debt, available cash, alerts, and transfers understood? Is a shortfall, reserve/debt tradeoff, or failed payment calling for an adjustment or assistance?

## C.R.A.F.T. audit

- **[C] Competence & Mastery:** What did I study or practice? What feedback changed my confidence or next learning step, and where did the idea transfer—or fail to transfer—to another case?
- **[R] Recovery & Physical Health:** How were sleep, tolerable movement, nourishment, symptoms, and access to care? Is a barrier or support need worth addressing without pushing beyond my capacity?
- **[A] Agents & Systems:** On comparable work, did automation help after setup, review, rework, failures, and cost? Did access, data, source and output checks work well enough; should I keep, change, or stop it?
- **[F] Faith & Ethics:** Which decision tested a commitment or relationship, who was affected or carried a burden, and is safe consultation, explanation, redress, or repair needed?
- **[T] Time Management & Planning:** When did focus and recovery fit or conflict with my obligations? Which interruptions were necessary or negotiable, and does the plan need a feasible adjustment?

**Safety and scope.** Seek urgent local help for an immediate safety threat. For health concerns, serious financial difficulty, or consequential decisions beyond your expertise, contact an appropriate qualified professional or support service. This framework cannot replace individualized care or resolve structural constraints.
